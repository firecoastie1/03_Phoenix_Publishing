Amelia

She should have been sleeping. She should have been lying on the narrow bed in the guest quarters, staring at a ceiling she didn't recognize, trying to process everything that had happened in the last twelve hours. Instead, she was sitting on the floor, it seemed, leaning against the wall, her legs stretched out in front of her, her hands in her lap, and trying very hard not to think about the man she had met exactly two days ago.

The bond was humming. Low. Steady. It wasn't as strong as it had been at the stream, it hadn't detonated, hadn't surged, but it was there, like a struck match that hadn't caught fire yet but was warm enough to burn if you touched it. She could feel him. Not his thoughts. Not his voice. Just his presence, somewhere in the compound, moving, working, carrying whatever weight he carried, and she could feel it through the bond. It was warm. It was heavy. It was him.

She pressed her palms against the floor. She had started doing that again, pressing her palms flat against whatever solid surface was closest, like it would ground her. It didn't. It never would. She had just realized, right then, that the only thing that grounded her was him, and he was somewhere else in this compound, and she wanted to go to him.

She stood up, smoothed her skirt, and walked to the door.

She didn't know why she was doing this. She knew one thing: she wasn't going to sit here and wait for Victor to come find her. If she wanted answers, she was going to have to get them herself. She was a person who asked questions. She was a person who needed to understand things. And right now, the thing she needed to understand most was the man who had looked at her at the stream and moved toward her like he'd been searching for her his whole life.

She opened the door.

---

The compound was alive, early morning, people moving, working, getting ready for whatever day the Alpha had planned. She walked toward the center of it, keeping to the edges of the paths, trying not to draw attention. But it was impossible. She was a human woman walking through a pack of shifters, and they noticed. She felt their eyes on her, not all of them, but enough. Some were curious, some were hostile, some were something else entirely. She kept her head up. She wasn't cowering. She wasn't running. She was a healer who had survived on her own for years, and she wasn't going to lose her nerve now.

A woman approached her first, dark hair, warm face, the kind of woman who carried herself like she was completely at home in a world full of predators. She stopped when she saw Amelia. Amelia tensed, then the woman smiled.

"You're the human," she said. Not a question. A statement. Her voice was warm, curious, nothing like the suspicion in some of the others' eyes. "I'm Emila. I'm in the healing tent."

Amelia was surprised. "I'm Amelia."

"I know." Emila's smile widened. "Victor talks about you, not much, obviously, but he talks. And we all know." She nodded toward the center of the compound. "He's in the war room. With Perseus. You can go find him if you want to."

Amelia blinked. "He... talks about me?"

Emila laughed, a soft, warm sound. "Don't let it go to your head. He talks about supply lines and patrol schedules and boundary markers. But sometimes... sometimes he talks about you." She paused, her eyes gentle. "He didn't come back alone. He came back carrying something. We can all feel it."

Amelia looked down at her hands. The bond was humming. "Thank you," she said. "Where is the war room?"

Emila pointed toward the center of the compound. "Two buildings down. You can't miss it. It's the one that looks like it has more people in it than the rest."

Amelia nodded. She started walking. She hadn't looked over her shoulder once, but she could feel the eyes still on her, watching, waiting, wondering what she was doing there.

A man approached her next, tall, broad-shouldered, with sharp features and a sharp look in his eyes. He stopped when he saw her. His mouth twisted, not a smile, not a frown, but something in between.

"You're back," he said. His voice was flat, not hostile, more skeptical. "I'm David. The one who made the joke about the stray." He crossed his arms. "You know, most humans would have run the other way after what happened. Most humans would be scared. You're just... walking around like you belong here."

Amelia was quiet for a moment. Then: "I don't belong here. But I need answers. And the only person who has any of them is Victor."

David stared at her. His mouth opened, then closed. He shook his head once, slow, and kept walking.

Amelia kept walking. She kept her head up. She wasn't cowering. She wasn't running. She was a person who needed answers, and she was going to get them.

Victor

He was sitting at the table in the war room looking at a map of the northern boundary and trying to focus on something other than the feeling in his chest.

The bond was still humming. It had been humming all night, he had tried to sleep, and every time he closed his eyes, he felt her. Not her thoughts. Not her voice. Her presence. It was like she was sitting in the room with him, even though he knew she was in the guest quarters, probably sleeping or trying to, probably thinking about the same thing he was thinking about.

He was angry. Not at her. At the situation. At the bond. At the fact that he was an Alpha who could not control his own body, could not control his own senses, could not control the one thing that mattered most, his ability to think clearly when she was in the same room as him.

"You look terrible," Perseus said. He was standing across the table from him, arms crossed, studying Victor the way he studied everything, carefully, calculatingly. "Have you slept?"

"No," Victor said. "She's been on my mind."

Perseus didn't comment. He just looked at the map. "The boundary is still thin. I've ordered additional patrols. If the Hollow King is.

The door opened.

Victor didn't turn. He didn't need to. The bond had flared the moment the door opened, sharp, immediate, undeniable. Like a struck match. And then, a second later, Amelia walked in.

She was standing in the doorway. Her hands were at her sides. Her posture was straight. Her face was calm. Not cowering. Not impressed. Just... there. Present. Observant. And the bond between them flared so hard it was almost painful.

David looked up from his place in the corner. Perseus turned his head. Josiah, who had been sitting quietly by the window, smiled.

"You were saying," Victor said. His voice was flat. He didn't look at Perseus. He couldn't look away from Amelia.

Perseus was quiet. Then: "I'll give you two a minute."

He didn't leave. Neither did David. Neither did Josiah. Victor didn't care. He was looking at Amelia, and she was looking at him, and the bond was humming between them, louder than it had been in the small room the night before, like something was waking up inside both of them.

"Amelia," he said. He didn't know why he had said her name. It just came out.

She walked toward him. She didn't rush. She didn't hesitate. She just walked, steady, like she had decided to do something and was going to follow through on it. When she reached him, she stopped in front of him, close enough that he could see the details of her face, close enough that he could feel the bond pulling toward her, close enough that he could smell the herbs on her skin.

"I need to talk to you," she said. Her voice was quiet. Steady. Not angry. Not scared. Just direct. She looked at the others in the room, then back at Victor. "Just us. No Josiah. No pack. No —" she gestured around the room. "None of this."

Victor was quiet. He was surprised. He didn't like being surprised. He liked being in control. He liked knowing what was going to happen next. But Amelia was standing in front of him, and she was looking at him like he was a puzzle she wanted to solve, and he didn't know how to handle it.

"What do you want to talk about?" he asked.

She was quiet for a moment. Then: "Us. The bond. Whatever it is. I felt something at the stream. I felt something when you touched me. I felt something when Josiah explained what it was. And I want to understand it. I'm not looking for a fantasy. I'm looking for truth."

Victor looked at her. He was looking at her face, the freckles across her nose, the slight furrow in her brow, the way her eyes were fixed on him like he was the only person in the room. He didn't know what to say to that. Because the truth was: he didn't understand it either. He was the Alpha. He was supposed to have answers. But this. This was something he couldn't calculate. Couldn't control. Couldn't explain.

"Let's walk," he said.

He didn't look at Perseus. He didn't look at David. He didn't look at Josiah. He just turned and started walking toward the door, and Amelia fell into step beside him.

They walked away from the compound. Away from the war room. Away from the pack. The bond hummed between them, steady and warm, and when they walked, their footsteps fell into sync without either of them noticing, left, right, left, right, matching without thinking, without planning, without trying. Victor didn't notice. Amelia didn't notice. But it happened.

They reached the edge of the compound. The trees were close, thick, dark, alive. The boundary was just beyond it. And they stopped. They stood in front of each other. The bond was humming. The forest was quiet.

"Go ahead," Victor said. He stepped back. He gave her space. "Tell me what you need to say."

She was quiet. She was looking at him, really looking at him, the way she had at the stream, the way she had in the small room with the fire, like she was trying to see past his face to whatever was underneath.

"I feel it too," she said finally. "The bond. I felt it at the stream, when you looked at me, when we touched. I felt something inside me wake up. And I don't know what it's waking up to. I know Josiah said it's not love. I know he said it's recognition. But I want to understand what that means. I want to know if this is just, she gestured at her chest, this pull. This tingling in my hands. This feeling like I'm standing too close to a speaker. Or if it's something more."

Victor was listening. He was looking at her. He didn't interrupt. He didn't try to fill the silence. He just let her talk, and the way she did it, direct, observant, present, made something warm and heavy settle in his chest.

"You're not afraid," he said.

She shook her head. "I should be. There are a lot of people in there who could probably kill me with their bare hands. Some of them looked at me like I was a threat. But I'm not afraid. Not of them. Not of you." She paused. "Not exactly.

He was quiet. He didn't know what to say to that. He was used to people being afraid of him, afraid of what he could do, afraid of his strength, afraid of his position. But Amelia was different. She wasn't afraid of him. She was just... there. Present. Observant. Looking at him like he was something worth understanding.

"Why aren't you afraid?" he asked.

She thought about it, then said, "Because you haven't given me a reason to be. You brought me back to the compound. You told Josiah I was a witness, not a stray. You explained the bond to me, even though I know you didn't want to. You told me the truth. You didn't try to trick me. You didn't try to scare me. You just... told me." She paused. "That matters."

Victor was quiet. He didn't know what to say to that. He was the Alpha. He was supposed to be commanding, decisive, in control. But Amelia was standing in front of him, and she was looking at him like he was just a person, and he didn't know how to handle it.

"Most humans would be terrified of me," he said.

She nodded. "Maybe. But you're not most humans. And I'm not most humans. I'm a healer. I see people at their most vulnerable: broken bones, deep cuts, people who can't lie because they're too weak to lie. I've learned to read them. And you, she paused. "You're trying so hard to control this. Your jaw is tight. Your hands are clenched at your sides. Your shoulders are tense. You're trying to control something you can't control, and it's making you uncomfortable. But you haven't done anything to hurt me. You've been careful. Patient. That tells me something."

Victor exhaled. He didn't know what he was exhaling. It was frustration. It was relief. It was something else, something he hadn't felt in a very long time, and he couldn't remember what it was called.

"You notice everything," he said.

"I'm a healer," she said. "I have to."

He was quiet. He was thinking about everything she had said, the truth, the recognition, the bond, the pull. He was thinking about the way his body had moved toward her at the stream. He was thinking about the fact that he hadn't thought about anything else since he'd seen her. Not supply lines. Not boundary markers. Not the missing hunter. Her.

"You're right," he said finally. "I'm trying to control something I can't control. And it's making me uncomfortable. But I won't pretend otherwise."

She nodded. "Good. I value honesty."

He was quiet. Then: "Do you want to understand the bond?"

"Yes."

"Do you want to know what it feels like?"

"Yes."

"Do you want to know what it means?"

She was quiet for a long moment. Then: "I want to know what it means for us. For me. For you. Not the Moon Goddess's strategy. Not Aurela's plan. But just, what does it mean that I can feel you? That you can feel me? What does it mean that I couldn't sleep last night because I felt your presence like you were sitting in the room with me? What does it mean that my pulse spikes when your voice drops? What does it mean that I haven't thought about anything else since I saw you?"

Victor stared at her. She had said it. She had said exactly what he had been thinking. And he didn't know what to say to that.

"I don't know," he said. "I don't know what it means. But I know one thing."

"What?"

"I haven't thought about anything else since I saw you either."

Something shifted in her expression. Not much. Just a small, almost imperceptible change, the way her eyes softened, the way her mouth relaxed, the way her shoulders dropped half an inch. Like she had been holding something tense and something inside her let it go.

"Okay," she said.

That was it. Just "okay." But it was enough. It was enough for now.

Victor nodded. "Okay."

The bond hummed between them. It was warm. It was steady. It was undeniable. And for the first time since the stream, since the tingling, since the moment he had looked at her and felt something shift in his chest, Victor felt something he couldn't name but knew was real.

It wasn't love. Not yet. Maybe not for a long time. But it was a beginning. And for the first time in a very long time, Victor didn't want to control it. He just wanted to follow it.

And that was enough.
