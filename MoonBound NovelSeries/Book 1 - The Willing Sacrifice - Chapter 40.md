# Chapter 40 — The Horizon

---

AMELIA

The morning light comes through the kitchen window the same way it always has. Dust in the air. The kettle on the stove. The compound alive outside with the sound of a pack learning how to breathe again. But something has shifted. Ever since the battle, after the barrier was raised, following the Hollow King's retreat across the Veil.

She is making tea. He is sharpening his knives. They are in the kitchen the way they have been every morning for weeks now. Not because they have to be together. Because they want to be.

She watches him from across the table. The way his jaw is set, focused on the steel. The way his shoulders are loose in a way she has never seen before. The way the morning light catches the silver in his hair that wasn't there before the war. She thinks about how close they came to losing all of this. To losing him. And she feels something in her chest tighten.

She reaches across the table. Her hand finds his. He doesn't look up, but his knife stops moving. His fingers turn, just slightly, until they are laced with hers. Warm. Solid. His.

She has been carrying something for weeks. She told herself it was stress. The exhaustion of healing a hundred wounded pack members while her own body screamed for rest. She told herself it was nothing. But last night, when she couldn't sleep, when the nausea came again and her hands shook holding her grandmother's mug, she knew.

She takes a breath. Victor's thumb moves over her knuckles. He is watching her now. Not the Alpha. With his own.

"I have something to tell you," she says.

He stops breathing.

She takes a breath. Looks at him. "I'm pregnant."

The knife hits the table. His eyes go wide. He doesn't say anything for a long moment. He just stares at her. At her stomach. At her face. At her. His hand closes over hers so hard she can feel his pulse hammering against her skin.

"A baby," he says. His voice is barely a whisper.

She nods. "A baby."

He stands up so fast his chair tips over. He doesn't notice. He pulls her out of her chair, into his arms, and she laughs — surprised, bright, real — and he spins her once, twice, and sets her down and presses his forehead to hers and whispers, "A baby."

She wraps her arms around his neck. "You're happy?"

He closes his eyes. His breath is shaky. "I am. I am. I am."

She rests her head on his shoulder. She can feel everything he's feeling: the terror, the joy, the impossibility of it, the way his chest feels too small to hold it all. She pushes back gently: the nausea at dawn, the exhaustion, the fear, the hope, the love, the terrifying, beautiful certainty that something new is growing inside her. He feels it. She feels him feel it. And for a long moment, they just stand there, holding each other, the morning light falling across the kitchen floor.

"We're going to be parents," she whispers.

"We're going to be parents."

He holds her. Long. Tight. Like he's memorizing the shape of her in his arms. And when he finally lets go, he picks up his chair, sets it back in place, sits down, takes her hand again, and says the quietest thing either of them has ever said:

"Our pup."

She smiles. The kind of smile that starts in the chest and spreads outward, slow and certain and full of everything they're afraid to hope for but can't stop believing in. "Ours."

He reaches across the table and takes her hand again. Neither of them lets go.

The kettle whistles. Neither of them moves. They just sit there, holding hands, in the kitchen, in the morning, with the sound of a living pack outside and something new growing inside her.

And it is enough.

---
VICTOR

He sits across from her. Her hand in his. The morning light falls across the table. Dust motes float in the air. The compound is alive outside, pack members training, children running, dogs barking. The sounds of a living, breathing pack.

A pup.

Their pup.

He feels the weight of what is about to happen, warm, real, unbreakable, and for the first time in his life, Victor understands what it means to have a future. Not just survival. Not just holding the line. A future.

He looks at her. Amelia. His mate. The woman who walked into shifter territory with nothing but a bag of herbs and a crescent moon tattoo on her wrist. The woman who healed his warriors when no one else would. The woman who stood beside him when the Hollow King came. The woman who never let him go.

A baby.

His baby.

The weight of it presses on him, not heavy, but real. Solid. Present. He feels it in his chest. In the way his hand closes around hers without thinking. He is going to be a father.

And he is not afraid.

# Epilogue

AMELIA

Weeks later, the pack is in better shape. The barrier holds. The Hollow King has not returned. The Blackwood compound is busy, pack members training, healers tending to injuries, children playing in the courtyard. Life is returning to normal. A new kind of normal, but a normal nonetheless.

Perseus comes to the compound gate with a message from the southern border. He finds Victor in the war room, looking over maps and patrol routes. Perseus hands him the letter without a word.

Victor opens it. Reads it. His expression does not change, but Perseus notices the way his fingers tighten around the paper.

"What is it?" Victor asks.

Perseus exhales slowly. "Hanley found something on their territory. Something that doesn't smell like wolf."

Victor's eyebrows rise. "What does it smell like?"

Perseus hesitates. Looks at him. "Panther."

The room goes quiet. Victor puts the letter down. Stands. Walks to the window. Looks south.

A panther pack.

One of the six shifter types Aurela mentioned. Not wolf. Not fox. Not bear. Panther. A different kind of shifter. A different kind of pack.

Victor does not say anything for a long moment. Then he turns back to Perseus.

"Did Gage see them?"

Perseus nods. "Hanley confirmed it. A small group of panthers, maybe thirty. They've been moving through the southern territories for months. Gage wants to reach out. He thinks they might be worth talking to."

Victor nods. "I will go."

Perseus stares at him. "You? The Alpha of the Blackwood Pack? You want to walk across hostile territory to meet strangers?"

Victor smiles. "I've learned something. Connection is stronger than fear. If we want to stop the Hollow King, really stop him, we need more than wolves. We need a coalition. All six types. All six mates."

Perseus considers this. Nods slowly. "When do we leave?"

"Tomorrow."

Perseus leaves. Victor stays in the war room. Looks at the map. South. To the panther pack. To the next mate. To the next bridge.

He is not afraid. He has his mate. He has his pack.

And he knows this is just the beginning.

---

AMELIA

She finds him in the war room later. He is still looking at the map. His jaw is set. His eyes are distant. She does not speak. Just stands beside him. Her hand on his shoulder.

He turns. Looks at her. Smiles.

"You're leaving," she says.

He nods. "Tomorrow. Perseus is going with me. We're going to meet a panther pack."

She nods. "How do you feel?"

He takes her hand. Squeezes. "Ready."

She smiles. "Good."

She kisses him. Slow. Soft. Sure. No magic. No goddess. Just them. Two people who have fought for each other. Who have chosen each other. Who have seen each other at their most vulnerable and have not looked away.

She steps back. Looks at her stomach. At the life growing inside her.

"I'm going to be a mother," she says.

He steps closer. Wraps his arms around her. One hand on her stomach. The other on her back. Presses his forehead to hers.

"Our pup," he says.

She smiles. "Ours."

They stand together in the war room. Looking at the map. Looking at the future. The Hollow King is out there. The Veil is still thin. The war is not over. But they are not afraid.

They have each other. They have a pack. They have a future.

And that is enough.
