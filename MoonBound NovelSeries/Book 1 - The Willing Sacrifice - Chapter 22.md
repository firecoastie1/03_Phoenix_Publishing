Chapter 22
The Suspects

AMELIA

She found him on the roof again.

She didn't announce herself. She just climbed up the access ladder and walked over. He was sitting on the edge, his head in his hands, his shoulders tense, his silence louder than any words he could have spoken. She sat down beside him, close enough to feel the warmth of his body but not close enough to touch. She didn't assume. He hadn't given permission.

He didn't look up. He didn't speak. He just sat there, staring at nothing.

She didn't ask how he was. He wasn't okay, and the question was too small for what he was carrying.

Instead she said: "The pack needs you."

And he said: "The pack doesn't need me to see what I've failed to protect."

She didn't argue. She just sat with him until he stopped shaking.

When he finally stood, she stepped back. He didn't say goodbye. He just climbed down the access ladder and walked away. She watched him go until he disappeared around the corner.

---

VICTOR

Perseus rode back into the compound at dusk. He was tired, his clothes dusty from the road, his expression unreadable. The Hanley negotiation had gone as well as could be expected, with Gage Hanley stubborn, traditional, and not impressed by Blackwood diplomacy. But Perseus had kept it from breaking down. Barely.

He didn't go to his quarters. He went straight to the healing tent. And that was where he found Marco standing at the edge of the tent, staring at something he couldn't bring himself to look at. He couldn't look away.

Marco's head snapped up when Perseus approached. He opened his mouth to speak, but no words came out. He just shook his head. He stepped aside, gesturing past him toward the cot.

Perseus looked past him.

Reid's face. Not Reid's body. His body was there, breathing, eyes open, chest rising and falling. But his eyes were empty. His mind was gone.

Hollowed.

Perseus stared at his younger brother, at the face he'd grown up with, the face he'd defended, the face he'd told to go south and open a tavern when Reid complained about pack life.

He didn't make a sound. He didn't move. He just stood there, his hands clenched into fists at his sides, his throat tight, his vision blurring.

Victor was there too, standing a few feet away, his face pale, his hands still. Victor had put Reid down. Victor had done it with his own hands.

Perseus looked at his Alpha, looked at his friend, and saw the weight of it. The crushing, immovable weight of a man who had put down someone he loved.

And then he turned and walked away. No words. No shouting. Just silence.

---

AMELIA

Perseus led the investigation. He was methodical, talking to every pack member who had been in the compound that day, checking the ward logs, reviewing the patrol routes. He didn't suspect anyone. Not yet. But he wasn't ruling anyone out either.

David wanted to punish everyone. He paced the war room, his fists clenched, his voice loud enough to shake the walls. "Lock them all down. Double the guards. Search every tent. If someone opened the wards, they'll be hiding something."

Josiah wanted to wait. He sat quietly in the corner, his hands folded in his lap, his eyes closed. "Panic is the Hollow King's first weapon," he said. "If we punish people for fear, we're giving him exactly what he wants."

Victor said nothing. He sat at the head of the table, his face unreadable, his silence louder than David's shouting. But Amelia could see his hands, resting flat on the table, trembling slightly, his knuckles white.

She watched the meeting from the door. She watched Perseus work. She watched David lose control. She watched Josiah sit calmly in the eye of the storm. And she watched Victor break, quietly, internally, completely.

When the meeting broke, she followed him outside.

He didn't notice her at first. He was walking toward the compound wall, his hands shoved in his pockets, his head down. She caught up to him and stepped into his path.

He stopped. He looked at her. His eyes were red-rimmed. His face was drawn. And he looked at her like he had nowhere left to go.

"You're punishing yourself," she said. "That doesn't help them."

He closed his eyes. His jaw tightened. When he opened them again, his voice was barely a whisper.

"I should have seen it. I should have been there. I should have—"

"You can't carry everything alone, Victor."

He didn't answer. He couldn't. He just looked at her with eyes full of everything he couldn't say.

And she knew then, not with magic, not with the bond, but with her own two eyes, that the Hollow King hadn't just hollowed Reid. He had hollowed Victor too. Not yet. But close.

---

VICTOR

He couldn't look at certain pack members without seeing Reid's face. Every time David laughed at something, a joke, a memory, something stupid and human and alive, it made his chest ache. Every time Marco walked past him, whistling some old tune, it made his hands shake. Every time Emila asked how he was holding up, and he said "fine," it made something inside him twist.

He was supposed to be the Alpha. The strong one. The one who held everything together. But all he could see was Reid's face. And all he could feel was the weight of a body he had put down.

He walked the perimeter of the compound alone. One step at a time. Eyes forward. Shoulders straight. But his hands were shaking, and his chest was tight, and the silence in his head was deafening.

He didn't know who had opened the wards. He didn't know who had let the Hollow King in. And he didn't know how to move forward when every step reminded him that he had failed, failed Reid, failed the pack, failed the person he was supposed to be.
