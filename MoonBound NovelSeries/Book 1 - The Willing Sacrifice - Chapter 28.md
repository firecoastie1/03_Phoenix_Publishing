# Chapter 28 — The Hanley Threat Grows

VICTOR

Morning reports. That's what they are. Routine. Bureaucracy disguised as survival. Victor sits at the head of the war room table, fingers steepled, eyes on Perseus's maps. David paces. Emila refills tea he doesn't drink. Josiah watches from the corner with eyes closed. Amelia is in the healing tent. He can feel her through the bond — a steady warmth, like a candle burning low. It keeps him grounded. Keeps him focused. He focuses on the reports. Northern border: clear. Supply runs: on schedule. New arrivals: three minor wolf families seeking shelter. He signs the papers. Nods. Says the right words. All the while, the bond hums in his chest, connecting him to her, to the pack, to the weight of a thousand decisions pressing down on his shoulders.

The meeting moves on. Supply inventories. Patrol rotations. Medical updates from Emila about the healing tent — herb stocks are low, but she's managing with what she has. He nods. Signs. Repeats. But his attention is divided. Half of him is here, in the war room. The other half is with her — in the healing tent, mixing herbs, feeling the bond like a thread pulling at his chest.

Then the door opens.

Perseus steps in. His expression has changed. The casual curiosity from yesterday is gone, replaced by a sharp, calculating intensity. He carries a rolled map in one hand and a leather pouch in the other. He sets them down on the table without ceremony and unrolls the map.

"We need to talk about Hanley," he says.

David stops pacing. Perseus's voice has that effect. The room goes quiet — even Josiah opens his eyes.

Victor leans forward. "What about him?"

Perseus unrolls the map across the table. His fingers trace a route along the southern border, past the tree line, into Shadowfen territory. "Three days ago, I went to the border to check the Hanley scout reports. What I found was worse than scouts." He taps the map. "There's a fortification. Half-built, but reinforced — palisade walls, watchtowers, supply caches. They're not pushing. They're planting flags."

David's jaw tightens. "How big?"

"Two hundred warriors. Maybe more. Gage has been building this in secret — moving supplies from the main camp under cover of night, using Hanley sympathizers in nearby packs to transport materials. He's been at this for months." Perseus pauses. "He's also recruited the Ironridge bears. Bram Stonefist is there. Two dozen bears."

The room goes very quiet.

Victor feels it — the weight of the news, the shift in the board. Wolves and bears. That's not harassment. That's preparation.

"How long?" Victor asks. "How long has it been there?"

Perseus taps a second point on the map. "About three weeks. The work started about a month ago. I missed it because Gage has eyes everywhere. But..." He pulls the leather pouch from his pocket and sets it on the table. "I found something else."

He opens the pouch. Inside: a handful of dried leaves, dark and brittle. He spreads them on the table.

"What are they?" Victor asks.

"Hollow King residue," Perseus says. "The same compound used in the hollowing process. Small amounts, but consistent. Gage isn't just building an army — he's hollowing his own warriors. Using the Hollow King's venom to make them fearless, relentless, unable to feel pain."

David's face goes white. Then red. "He's arming himself with *that*? In our territory?"

"Not our territory," Perseus corrects. "Shadowfen. But it's overlapping. And if he pushes further, it will be ours. Gage is calling the Blackwood weak — saying we hesitated, that we won't fight back. He says our Alpha bonded with a human like a coward hiding behind magic instead of facing the Hollow King directly." He meets Victor's gaze. "He's using the bond against us. Calling it proof that you've lost your edge, Victor."

Victor feels the bond tighten in his chest. Amelia's warmth flickers — concern, sharp and sudden. He sends calm back through the bond without thinking. *I'm fine.* The warmth settles. He keeps his face neutral.

"Does anyone else know about the hollowing?" Victor asks.

Perseus shakes his head. "Only Gage's inner circle. Darius Voss is running it. I got close enough to smell it, and close enough to see the warriors. They're... not right. Their eyes are wrong. The way they move. No hesitation. No fear. Like puppets on strings."

David slams his fist on the table. "We can't just sit here and let him build an army on our border. We have to hit him now — before he's ready. A demonstration force. Send a squadron to the fortification, show him that the Blackwood Pack is not going to back down." He turns to Victor. "Alpha."

Victor holds his brother's gaze. He can feel the weight of the room — David's anger, Perseus's calculation, Josiah's quiet attention, Emila's silence. The bond hums in his chest, connecting him to Amelia, to the pack, to the weight of a decision that will shape everything.

He considers it. The logic is sound. A demonstration force would show Hanley that the Blackwood is not weak. It would deter further expansion. But it would also be the first strike — the spark that ignites a war. And the Hollow King is still breathing down his neck. If he fights Hanley, he divides his forces. He opens himself to attack from the Hollow King's shadows.

But if he does nothing, Hanley sees it as weakness. He grows bolder. And eventually, he comes for the Blackwood regardless.

"There's more," Perseus says quietly. "Hanley is not the only threat. Darius Voss has been communicating with the Hollow King — in secret. I didn't see the communication, but I saw the traces. The same residue from the fortification is on the supply routes. Gage thinks he's playing a game — wolves versus wolves, territory versus territory. But the Hollow King is playing him. He's using Gage to weaken both packs so he can pick up the pieces."

Victor closes his eyes. The pieces are falling into place, and none of them fit.

Hanley is building an army with Hollow King venom. The Hollow King is using Hanley as a pawn. And in the middle of it all, his pack is watching him, waiting for the Alpha to make the right call.

He opens his eyes. "David's right. We can't back down." He looks at Perseus. "But a demonstration force is too aggressive — too close to the first strike. Send a smaller group. Not warriors. Scouts. Ten men. Go to the border, show them we see what they're doing. Tell Gage that the Blackwood Pack is aware of his fortifications, and that we would prefer not to discuss them with blood." He pauses. "If he attacks them, we respond. If he doesn't, we have time to prepare."

David shakes his head. "You're sending *scouts*? Ten? Against two hundred warriors and bears?"

"Ten is enough to show we're not afraid," Victor says. "And it's not enough to be a threat. It's a message, not a battle."

Perseus considers this. Then he nods. "I'll pick the scouts. And I'll lead them." He looks at David. "We're not looking for a fight. We're looking for a statement."

David clenches his jaw. He doesn't like it. But he nods. Reluctantly. "Understood."

Josiah speaks from the corner, his voice quiet but carrying. "Gage will not listen to reason. He sees the bond as weakness because he does not understand it. He thinks love makes you soft. He does not understand that love makes you fearless." He meets Victor's gaze. "You are fearless because you have something worth protecting. That is what he fears."

Victor holds his gaze. "What should I do?"

Josiah's eyes close again. "You do what you have always done. Lead. Protect. Trust your pack. The bond does not make you weak, Victor. It makes you more than one."

Victor nods. The words settle in his chest, warm and steady. He sends them through the bond to Amelia — not the words, just the feeling. Gratitude. Resolve. The warmth in the bond brightens in response.

---

DAVID

David stands at the window of the war room, watching the scouts prepare. Ten warriors. His jaw aches from clenching it. He chose them himself — Perseus made him choose, and David picked the best. Marcus Chen, who never flinches. Tessa Okafor, who has eyes like a hawk. Jalen Brooks, who can shift blind if he has to. They are the best the pack has. And Victor is sending them to stare down two hundred hollowed warriors and twenty bears.

*It is not enough.* The thought repeats in his head like a drumbeat. *It is never enough.*

He remembers the first war. The one before Victor became Alpha. His father — Torren Beaumont — led a full battalion against a rival pack that had grown too bold. They won. They buried three hundred dead in mass graves. He remembers the weight of the silence afterward. The way the pack had felt hollow, even in victory.

Torren died two years later. Hollow King venom in his chest. Not in battle. In betrayal. His own lieutenant, selling them out for a promise of power. David has never forgiven himself for not seeing it coming.

Now Victor is doing the same thing. Sending a handful of scouts to challenge an enemy that could crush them without thinking. *And he calls it strength.*

David's hands curl into fists. He turns away from the window. Walks back to the table. Picks up his cup. Sets it down. Picks it up again. Emila watches him with knowing eyes.

"You are going to wear a hole in the floor," she says quietly.

David forces a smile. "I know."

"It is what you do."

He looks at her. "It is what I *want* to do. Not what I am allowed to do."

She pours him more tea. "You want to protect your brother. That is not weakness. That is love. Josiah was right."

David sits. Stares at the table. Thinks about the bond. Thinks about Amelia. The human woman who walked into a pack of shifters and made Victor *better*. He is still fighting that — not the bond itself, but what it represents. What it makes Victor. Softer. Open. Transparent. The pack can *feel* him now. They can feel his happiness, his fear, his doubt. And the pack is a strange, suspicious thing. They do not trust what they can feel. They trust what they can predict.

Victor has become unpredictable.

David takes a breath. Sets his cup down. Stands. Walks to the door. Stops. Looks back at Perseus, checking the scouts' gear. At Josiah, sitting in the corner with his eyes closed. At Emila, watching him with gentle patience.

He opens his mouth. Closes it.

"Perseus," he says.

Perseus looks up.

"If anything goes wrong with those scouts—"

"I know the rules, David. I am not going to throw my life away."

David nods. He should walk away. He should trust his brother. He should trust Perseus.

But he doesn't.

---

PERSEUS

Perseus watches the scouts leave. Ten warriors moving south along the old trade road, carrying only supplies and word. No weapons beyond their belts. No armor beyond their hides. They are brave. They are skilled. But they are ten against two hundred.

He feels the weight of the decision — Victor's decision. Not a full strike. Not silence. Something in between. A statement. A message. *We see you. We are not afraid. Do not test us.*

It is the right call. Perseus knows this. He also knows that messages do not stop armies. Intentions do not stop hollowed warriors. And Gage Hanley does not care about messages.

But Victor is the Alpha. The call is his. Perseus's job is to execute it — and to prepare for what happens next.

He turns toward the armory. Picks up a sword. Tests its weight. The battle is coming. It always is. The question is not *if* — it is *when* and *how hard*.

He goes to the training yard. Begins to move.

The work begins.
