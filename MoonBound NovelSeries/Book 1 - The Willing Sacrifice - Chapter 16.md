# Chapter 16: The Pack Reacts

Victor

The second ring of the bell shattered everything.

Every conversation in the courtyard stopped. Boots hit gravel. Doors slammed open. People poured out of the halls like water breaking a dam, running toward the center, toward the healing tent, toward whatever sound had just split the morning in two.

Victor moved first. He crossed the compound in three long strides, his boots heavy on the packed earth, pulling Amelia along beside him, and reached the healing tent before most of the pack had even stopped running.

Samuel stood at the tent entrance, arms crossed, jaw clenched, watching with the kind of cold focus that came from years of patrol. He did not approach. He did not need to. His body said *stay back* with the same authority Victor used for pack decrees.

"Get out," Victor said to the crowd that had gathered at the tent edge. "Everyone out. Now."

No one moved at first. The pack had heard the bell. They had seen Marcus being brought in bound and breathing and *wrong*. Now they were seeing him thrashing and making that sound, and something primal had woken in their blood. Fear was not a shifter trait. It was the one trait that made them human. And it was spreading.

"David," Victor said.

David was already moving, his good arm out, pushing people back, herding them toward the compound center. "Move. Everyone to the meeting ground. Now."

Victor ducked under the tent flap and went inside. Amelia followed close behind.

"Victor." Amelia's voice was steady. "The restraints are holding. But he is not going to stop. Not on his own."

"He knows Marcus," Victor said. "Or he knew him. His body does not."

"I know." Amelia's fingers tightened on the floor. "His body is moving. But the person inside is gone. All I can find is breath and pulse and no one home."

Victor felt the thread between them tighten, warmth, awareness, the quiet hum of a connection that was still a thread. He could feel her steadiness. It grounded him. He stepped closer, stood behind her, let his hand rest on her shoulder. She leaned back into him, just for a second, just enough.

Then the sound stopped.

The bound figure went still. The grinding noise cut out. His body went limp. His eyes stayed open, staring at nothing.

The silence that followed was worse than the noise.

Amelia let out a breath she had been holding. "He's done," she said.

"Get him released," Victor told Samuel outside. "Put him in the east cell. Double restraints. No one approaches alone."

Samuel nodded once, already moving to fetch the key.

"Alpha?" One of the younger warriors stepped forward. A boy, really. Early twenties, lean, still filling out his frame. Grayden. "What do we do now? We can't keep —" He gestured at the tent. At the bound figure. At the hollowed.

"We do what we have always done," Victor said. "We hold the line. We do not hide. We do not retreat behind wards that were never meant to keep the world out. The pack stands together, or the pack does not stand at all. Clear?"

A murmur moved through the crowd. Some heads nodded. Some did not.

Perseus stepped into the clearing. His face was unreadable, his posture relaxed, but his eyes were sharp. He looked at the tent, at the crowd, at Victor, and he did not miss anything.

"Victor," he said, low enough that only Victor heard. "The war room. Now."

Victor looked at Amelia. She was still sitting on the floor. Her hands were steady. Her face was calm. But he could feel the tremor in her pulse, not fear. Empathy. She felt what the pack was feeling. The raw, unfiltered fear of a group of people who had just seen something that broke every rule of how the world was supposed to work.

"Stay here," Victor said.

Amelia shook her head. "I'm coming."

---

The war room was a long table with a territory map spread across it, shelves of supplies on the walls, and windows looking out over the northern boundary. Victor sat at the head. Perseus took the chair to his right. David stood at the window, arms crossed, staring north. Josiah sat in the corner chair, still and quiet, watching everything.

The pack filed in behind them, twenty-seven members, all the adults, all the warriors, all the Omegas who had opinions worth hearing. They took seats around the table or stood along the walls. The mood was tense. Not hostile. Not yet. But the kind of tension that preceded either courage or panic, and nobody knew which way it would break.

Amelia took a seat at the far end of the table, next to Emila, who pulled her chair closer without a word. Josiah caught Amelia's eye and nodded, small, deliberate, the kind of nod that said *you belong here* without speaking it aloud. Amelia felt something shift in her chest, not the bond, just the quiet certainty that she was being seen.

Victor stood at the head of the table. He did not need to raise his voice. He did not need to command attention. The room went quiet on its own.

"The hollowed warrior we found this morning," Victor began. "Marcus LaBlanc. He was missing. He is back. Not as himself. Something or someone took him and brought him back empty. His mind, his memories, his emotions. All gone. His body is alive. His heart is beating. But he is not Marcus anymore."

Silence. Heavy. Uncomfortable.

David spoke first. "We need to hit harder. Now. Whatever is out there, we find it and we break it before it breaks us."

Perseus did not look at him. He looked at Victor. "We need information. Before we make moves. We do not know what we are facing. We do not know how many hollowed there are. We do not know if Marcus was the first."

Josiah spoke quietly. "He was not. The old records say the Hollow King does not strike in one place at one time. He spreads. He tests. He waits to see where the pack is weakest. And he finds it."

The room shifted. Some faces tightened. Some looked down. Fear was no longer abstract. It had a face. It had a name. Marcus.

Victor looked at David. "I hear you. I want him gone as much as you do. But we do not charge blind. Not anymore."

David's jaw clenched. He wanted to argue. He wanted to run at the sky and tear it apart. But he held back. For now.

Perseus leaned forward. "Victor. What if this is not just a wolf territory problem? What if the Hollow King is targeting all shifter types? We know he is. We have records. The patterns match. Every time a bond forms, he strikes. And this time, the bond has just formed. We are the first test."

Victor was quiet. He was looking at the territory map. At the lines that divided pack boundaries. At the gaps between territories. At the places where the Veil was thin.

"Then we investigate," he said at last. "Not just the northern boundary. Every boundary. Every territory. We send teams to check the other packs. Panthers. Bears. Foxes. Eagles. Dragons. We find out if this is happening to all of them or just us."

Perseus nodded once. "I will organize teams. Two per territory. We leave within three days."

David spoke again, quieter this time. "What if the Hollow King follows them?"

"Then we make sure they are strong enough to handle it," Victor said.

A young warrior at the far end of the table, Grayden, David's mirror image, young and eager, spoke up. "Alpha — what about the wards? We should strengthen the wards. Make them impenetrable. Nothing gets in."

David shot him a look. "We are not a fortress. We are a pack."

"We should be both," Grayden shot back.

Victor raised a hand. The room went quiet again.

"The wards hold the Hollow King at bay. That is what they are for. We will reinforce them. But we do not hide behind them. If we spend all our energy building walls, we forget that we have weapons. And we are not defenseless."

The room settled. Not perfectly. Not with unanimous agreement. But with enough understanding for now.

Josiah leaned forward. "Victor has spoken. The pack does not hide. The pack does not retreat. We investigate. We prepare. We hold the line. And we do not forget that the Hollow King feeds on fear. Fear makes him stronger. Unity makes us stronger. Do not let him win that battle before the first arrow is shot."

The pack nodded. Some louder than others. But it was enough.

Victor nodded back once. "Dismissed." He turned and left the war room, Perseus falling into step beside him as the pack began to break.

---

After the meeting, the compound broke into its normal rhythm: patrols, training, meals, the steady hum of a community that refused to stop living just because something dark was circling.

Amelia walked home through the compound. She moved past the training yard where David was pushing warriors through combat drills, past the kitchen where Marco was arguing with the cook about spices, past the healing tent where Emila was setting up supplies for the evening. Everyone was busy. Everyone was doing their job. And under it all, she could feel the fear, quiet, held in, not spoken aloud, but present like a low-voltage current running through the pack's veins.

She had spent her entire life watching people. It was what healers did. You observed before you acted. You measured before you touched. And the pack was no different from a patient: you could see the tension in their shoulders, the tightness around their eyes, the way some people moved too fast while others moved too slow. Fear made people unpredictable. That was the dangerous part.

Josiah passed her in the path between the archive and the compound center. He did not slow down, but he did glance at her, just a flicker of his eyes, just a nod, just a *I see you*.

She stopped. "I was just watching."

Josiah did not look surprised. "You are a healer. Observation is your craft."

"I'm trying to understand."

Josiah smiled faintly. "Understanding is its own craft. You are doing well."

He moved on. She stood there for a moment, processing what he had said. *You are doing well.* That was not something she expected to hear from an Elder who had lived through centuries of pack politics and supernatural warfare and mate bonds and hollowed warriors. But it was enough to steady her.

She kept walking.

The compound gave way to the path that led to the edge of the territory, past the boundary stones, past the tree line, past the place where the forest opened into the meadow and the meadow gave way to the road and the road went to town. The air changed as she moved north. The Veil was thinner here. She could feel it, not like a wall or a barrier, exactly. More like standing too close to something massive and alive, like the air itself was vibrating. Cold water against her skin. A pressure on her chest that had nothing to do with the bond.

She looked up.

The sky looked wrong for a moment.

Not cloudy. Not storming. Just... wrong. The sky was shifting, like someone had rearranged the stars while she was not looking. The angles were off. The spacing was wrong. It lasted maybe three seconds. Then it passed, and the sky looked normal again.

Her hands started tingling.

Not from the Veil. From him.

Victor.

The pull was strong. Not the desperate, pulling sensation from before. More like a steady hum, a current running under her skin, saying *he is there, he is safe, he is thinking about you*. She could not read his thoughts, the thread was not open, but she could feel the shape of his presence, warm and steady and familiar, like a note held in the air.

She stood there at the edge of the territory, hands tingling, Veil pressing against her skin, stars rearranging and then not, and she thought: *This is what the world looks like now. This is what I live in.*

She did not hate it.

She kept walking.
