Chapter 25
The Decision

VICTOR

He called the pack at dawn. Not a council. Not a meeting. A gathering. Every member of the Blackwood Pack was expected to be there. Inner council, warriors, healers, scouts, cooks, guards. Everyone.

David arrived first, arms crossed, jaw tight. He'd been waiting for Victor to make a move. David always wanted Victor to make a move.

Perseus arrived second, his face unreadable, his findings in hand. Victor tore through them. The math was clean. The Hollow King had done it alone. The wards hadn't been broken from the inside. They'd been dissolved from the outside.

Josiah arrived third, his staff in hand, his eyes calm. He'd seen this before. Not the Hollow King. Not exactly. But the shape of what was coming. The weight of it. The necessity of the choice Victor was about to make.

Emila arrived fourth. She smiled at Victor when she saw him, a small thing, but it meant something. She always smiled at him. It reminded him that not everyone in this pack was afraid. Some people trusted him. That was a weight too.

The rest came in waves. Dozens of them. Then hundreds. They filled the central yard, standing shoulder to shoulder, looking up at Victor on the steps of the main building. The morning light was pale, thin, the way it was in late autumn. The air was cold. Breath hung in it.

Victor stood on the steps and looked out at them. At his pack. At the lives that depended on him. At the people he'd sworn to protect. At the people he'd failed.

Marcus. And Reid.

He pushed the names down. They sat in his chest like stones, but he pushed them down. He couldn't carry them right now. Not here.

"I called you here," he said. His voice carried. It always did. "Because I owe you the truth."

The yard went still. Even the wind seemed to stop.

"Something got through the wards," he continued. "Two of our own. Marcus at the boundary. Reid inside the compound. Someone I trained with. Someone I called my friend. I put them both down."

A murmur went through the yard. He could hear it. Quiet, pained, the kind of sound a pack makes when something sacred is taken from them. He let it happen. Let them feel it. Then he kept going.

"The investigation thought it was betrayal. Someone inside the walls. Someone who opened the door. That was wrong."

He looked at Perseus. Perseus nodded once.

"The Hollow King opened the door," Victor said. "He dissolved the wards from the outside. His Void Power. He doesn't need help. He doesn't need a traitor. He can come back. Any time."

A larger murmur. This one louder. This one edged with fear. Fear was the Hollow King's weapon. Victor knew that now. He could feel it radiating off the crowd like heat off stone.

"He can enter minds," Victor said. "He can manipulate fear. He can turn what you're afraid of against you. He's not a monster in the woods. He's a predator of the mind."

David stepped forward. His face was red, his hands clenched. "So what do we do? Do we fortify? Do we reinforce the wards? Do we find him and kill him?"

"We can't reinforce them," Perseus said from the steps beside Victor. "Not if he can dissolve them from the outside. Reinforcing just gives him more to dissolve."

"Then what?" David's voice was sharp. "What's the plan?"

Josiah stepped forward. He leaned on his staff, his eyes on the crowd, his voice soft but carrying. "There is no plan. Not yet. There is only a strategy. And a strategy requires a choice."

Victor looked at him. Josiah's eyes met his. There was nothing in them that asked him to rush. Nothing that asked him to be afraid. Just patience. Just trust.

"I know Aurela," Victor said. The name rippled through the crowd. Some knew it. Some didn't. All of them felt it. The Moon Goddess. The one who created their kind. The one who had been silent for centuries. "She speaks to me. In dreams. In visions. She told me why the Mate Bond exists. It is not for romance. It is not for connection. It is a weapon. A barrier. Bonds between different shifter types create walls the Hollow King cannot cross. My bond to Amelia. A human. A shifter. Is the first wall. It is not enough. But it is real."

He paused. He looked out at them. At his people. At their fear, their doubt, their need for certainty.

"I am not asking you to believe in a goddess," he said. "I am asking you to believe in me."

Silence.

Then Josiah stepped forward and knelt. Just one knee. Just one. But it was enough. He placed his staff on the ground. He looked up at Victor with eyes that had seen centuries.

"I believe," he said.

David hesitated. He looked at Josiah. He looked at Victor. He looked at Perseus, who gave a slow nod. Then David stepped forward and knelt. One knee.

"I believe," he said.

One by one, they followed. Warriors. Scouts. Healers. Guards. Dozens of them. Some knelt. Some bowed their heads. Some just stood there, eyes on Victor, waiting for him to say what came next.

And then Victor said it.

"I will complete the bond."

The word landed like a stone in still water. Ripples. Then silence.

"I will perform the Moonlit Rite. The ceremony. Under the next full moon."

He didn't look away. He held their eyes.

"Not because Aurela told me to. Not because it is the right strategic move. I will do it because Amelia is my mate. And I refuse to let fear dictate my life. The ceremony is not just magic. It is a vow. And I am ready to make it."

The yard erupted. Voices, shouts, the murmur of a pack processing something enormous. Victor didn't move. He stood on the steps, his hands at his sides, his face steady, his heart hammering against his ribs.

Perseus was the first to reach him. He put a hand on Victor's shoulder. His voice was low. "Are you sure?"

Victor didn't hesitate. "Yes."

Perseus nodded. "Then we prepare. The ceremony requires preparation. Time. Space. Josiah will need room."

Victor looked at Josiah. Josiah was watching him. Waiting.

"How long?" Victor asked.

Josiah thought for a moment. "Three days. The next full moon is three days from tonight. We have time."

Victor nodded. "Then three days."

He looked out at the yard one more time. At the faces. At the fear that was slowly turning into something else. Something steadier. Something that looked like hope.

It was his. Not Aurela's. Not the Moon Goddess's. His. He had chosen it.

AMELIA

Amelia was in the garden when Josiah came to find her. She was sitting on the bench where they'd met before, watching the flowers bloom in the thin morning light, her hands folded in her lap, the bond humming steady in her chest.

Josiah sat beside her. He didn't say anything at first. He just sat. He was patient that way.

"They heard," he said finally.

She looked at him. "About what?"

"About the ceremony. Victor's decision."

She felt it before she heard it. The bond flaring, not with warmth, not with pulse, but with something larger. Certainty. A kind of weight that settled into her bones and made her feel both steady and terrified.

"He said what?" she asked.

"He said he will complete the bond. The Moonlit Rite. Under the next full moon."

Three days. The moon was waxing. Three nights from now, it would be full again. The same moon that had drawn them to the clearing. The same moon that had hummed between them like a live wire.

She looked at Josiah. He was watching her. His eyes were kind.

"What do you want to do?" he asked.

It was a simple question. But it was enormous. It was the kind of question that changed lives.

She thought about her grandmother's voice. About the way she taught her to make tinctures, to mix herbs, to trust her hands. "The body knows what it needs," she used to say. "Your job is to listen."

She listened.

The bond hummed. Warm. Steady. Pulling toward Victor like gravity. She didn't fight it. She never did.

"I want to," she said, then caught herself. She met Josiah's eyes. "I'm ready to do it. Whatever it takes."

Josiah smiled. It was a small smile. But it was real. "Then we prepare."

She turned back toward the healing tent. She had things to do. Herbs to prepare. Supplies to gather. Her grandmother's packet was in her pack. She could use it tonight. She didn't know what she was doing. But she was going to try.
