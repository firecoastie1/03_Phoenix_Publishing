# Chapter 29 — The Confrontation

VICTOR

Evening settles over the compound. The war room is quiet. Papers stacked, maps rolled, lamps lit. Victor stays behind after the others have left. He sits at the head of the table, staring at the same report he has not read in ten minutes. The bond hums in his chest — Amelia is in the healing tent, asleep, her warmth steady and slow. It grounds him. But it does not quiet his thoughts.

The door opens.

David walks in. No knocking. He closes the door behind him. The click of the latch is loud in the empty room. He does not sit. He stands on the other side of the table, arms crossed, jaw tight. His eyes are red. Not from crying. From exhaustion. From holding something in.

"You need to talk to me," David says.

Victor closes the report. Sets it down. Looks at his brother. "What is it?"

"It is the Hanley situation. And it is not." David shakes his head. He runs a hand through his hair. Paces two steps. Stops. "You sent ten scouts against two hundred warriors and bears. Ten." His voice is low. Controlled. But it is vibrating at the edges.

"I said it was not a full strike. It is a message."

"It is a death sentence if Hanley attacks them." David's hands curl into fists. "You know that. You know Gage does not listen to messages. He listens to force. And you sent ten men to deliver one."

Victor holds his gaze. "It is the right call."

"It is not!" David slams his hand on the table. "It is not the right call. It is the call of a man who has forgotten what war looks like. Who thinks love makes him wise. Love makes you cautious. Love makes you soft. And the pack does not need soft right now, Victor. The pack needs a warrior."

Victor stands. Slowly. The movement is deliberate. Controlled. He walks around the table until he is standing in front of his brother. Close enough to see the pulse beating in David's neck. Close enough to feel the heat radiating off him. Close enough to smell the anger.

"Sit down," David says quietly.

"I am not going to sit."

"Then listen." David's voice is steady now. The anger has burned down to something sharper. Clearer. "I respect you, Victor. I always have. Since we were children, since Father made us train, since he made me watch you take every blow so I could learn. I have followed you into every fight. I have stood behind you when you became Alpha. I have bled for this pack. And I am telling you — right now — that you are making a mistake. Not just with Hanley. With everything."

Victor waits.

David takes a breath. "The bond. The way you carry it. The way you look at her. The way you let it change you." He shakes his head. "You are the Alpha. You can't afford to be... attached to anything. To anyone. Your pack needs you focused. Present. Not distracted by some... some human who walks into our compound and turns our Alpha into a lovesick fool."

Victor feels the bond tighten in his chest. Amelia stirs in her sleep. She feels the spike in his pulse, the heat in his blood. He sends calm through the bond without thinking. *It is fine. It is just David.*

"She is not a distraction," Victor says. His voice is quiet. Low. The kind of quiet that means he is holding something back.

"She is everything." David meets his gaze. "And that is the problem. You are not the Alpha anymore. You are a mate. And the pack knows it. They can feel you through the bond. They feel your happiness. Your fear. Your doubt. They do not know what to do with it. A controlled Alpha is predictable. An open Alpha is... unsettling."

Victor feels the truth of it in his chest. The pack is watching. They are always watching. And now they see something different in him. Something they do not understand.

"I am the Alpha," Victor says. His voice does not rise. It does not need to. "I decide what I can afford. I decide what this pack needs. Not you. Not Perseus. Not the pack. Me."

David stares at him. The anger flickers — something raw and wounded underneath. "Father would not have—"

"Father is dead." Victor's voice is flat. Final. "And I am not him. I lead differently. I lead better. And if the pack cannot accept that, then they need to learn to."

David shakes his head slowly. He does not look away. He does not back down. But he nods. Once. Reluctant. Resigned. "Understood."

He turns. Walks to the door. Stops. Does not look back. "Just know this: if your love gets those scouts killed, you will carry it. Every one of them. Marcus. Tessa. Jalen. You will carry them."

The door closes behind him. The click of the latch is loud in the empty room.

Victor stands alone.

He closes his eyes. Sends the weight of his brother's words through the bond to Amelia — not the words, just the feeling. Conflict. Tension. Fear. The warmth in the bond brightens in response. She is awake now. Listening.

*I'll be okay,* he thinks. *We will be okay.*

She feels him. She feels the shape of his love. And it is enough.

---

DAVID

David walks the corridors of the compound in silence. The stone floor is cold beneath his boots. The lamps cast long shadows against the walls. He can hear the compound settling around him — the murmur of voices, the creak of timber, the distant bark of a dog. Life. Normal life. For people who are not standing at the edge of a war they do not yet understand.

He stops at the window of the corridor. Looks out into the darkness. The compound is quiet. Peaceful. He hates it.

It is not the Hanley situation that is eating at him. It is not the ten scouts. It is not even the bond itself. It is what the bond represents. It is what it has taken from Victor. The man who stood at the head of the war room table, eyes red, jaw clenched, telling his brother *I lead differently. I lead better.* He was right. David knows he was right. And that is what makes it worse.

Because Victor is right because of her. Because of Amelia. Because of the bond.

And David knows — deep in his chest — that Victor has not just changed. He has become better. Stronger. Clearer. The man who walked into the war room tonight was not the man who left it two days ago. The ceremony changed him. The bond changed him. And David is not sure if he likes what he sees.

Because the man he sees is a better Alpha. And he is not sure if a better Alpha is what the pack needs.

He remembers the first war. The one before Victor became Alpha. His father was a good Alpha. Strong. Fierce. Relentless. But he was also alone. His own Alpha had no one to lean on. No one to trust. He carried the weight of the pack on his shoulders and did not let anyone else feel it. That is what made him strong. That is what made him predictable.

And that is what killed him.

David closes his eyes. The door opens behind him. He does not turn.

"David?"

He knows the voice. Perseus. His brother's other half. The one who has always been the voice of reason. The one who has always known better than to follow impulse.

"I know you are out there," Perseus says.

David turns. Perseus stands in the corridor, arms crossed, expression unreadable. He does not approach. He waits.

"I am fine," David says.

Perseus studies him. He is good at that. Reading people. Reading situations. "You just confronted your brother."

"I told him the truth."

"He does not need truth. He needs support."

"Sometimes the truth is the support." David looks away. Looks back at the darkness outside the window. "He is making a mistake, Perseus. And I know it. And I know he is right. And that is what terrifies me."

Perseus is quiet for a long moment. Then he speaks. "You think love makes him soft."

"I think love makes him— " David stops himself. Shakes his head. "I don't know what it makes him. But I know this: the pack is watching. And if they see weakness, they will test him. And if he breaks, the Hollow King will be waiting."

Perseus nods slowly. "You want to fight him."

"I want to protect him." David turns away. Looks at his brother. Really looks at him. "I have always wanted to protect him. Since we were children. And I think I have been bad at it because I have only ever protected him from the outside. From the pack. From the enemies. But what if the real threat is what is inside him? What if the bond is the thing that gets him killed?"

Perseus meets his gaze. "Then you ask yourself: did love get him the victory in Ch 16? Did love get him through the hollowing? Did love get him the respect of the Ironridge bears?" He pauses. "The bond does not make him weak, David. It makes him more than one."

David laughs. A short, bitter sound. "Since when do you sound like Josiah?"

"Since I started listening to him."

David looks away. Thinks about it. The corridor is quiet. The compound sleeps. And somewhere in the compound, his brother is carrying the weight of a pack, a bond, and a war.

"I just want him to survive," David says quietly.

"So do I," Perseus says. "So do I."

They stand in silence. Two brothers. One pack. One war. And the thin, invisible line that separates love from destruction.
