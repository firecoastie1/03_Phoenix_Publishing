Chapter 21
The Second Attack

AMELIA

She was in the compound when it happened.

Not by invitation. Not by plan. She'd come to return something, one of her herbal notes that Victor had picked up in the war room weeks ago. She didn't even remember why she'd thought to bring it back. Maybe because she'd noticed he'd been using it as a bookmark for his patrol reports. Maybe because it felt like a small, stupid thing that made her feel useful.

She was sitting in the healing tent talking to Emila about wound care when the alarm sounded.

It wasn't a horn or a shout. It was the way the air changed, a pressure drop, like the moment before a storm. Emila stopped mid-sentence. Her head tilted. Her eyes went wide.

"I feel it too," she said quietly.

She didn't know what had happened, but she didn't need to. She just got up and ran toward the healing tent.

---

VICTOR

He felt it first as a pressure behind his eyes. Then a cold wave, like ice water poured down his spine. Then the smell, not blood, not fire, but something worse. Something empty. Something that smelled like absence.

He was in his quarters, sitting at his desk, when Marco burst through the door.

"Alpha," he said, and his voice was shaking. "The healing tent. Someone — I don't — I can't—"

Victor was already moving. He was out of the door, down the hall, across the compound, before he knew what he was doing.

He found Marco at the edge of the healing tent, looking at something on the ground that he couldn't look at. He couldn't look away. He was staring at a face he knew. A face he'd grown up with. A face he'd laughed with, fought with, bled with.

Marco's face. Not Marco's body. His body was there, breathing, eyes open, chest rising and falling. But his eyes were empty. His mind was gone.

Hollowed.

Victor recognized him. It was Kael. A warrior he'd trained with since they were pups. A man who'd stood beside him during his first pack council. A man who'd joked about retiring to the southern border and opening a tavern.

Kael was reaching for him. Movement mechanical and wrong, like a puppet with strings cut halfway.

Victor didn't want to. He couldn't. But he had to. He put him down.

He didn't cry. He didn't make a sound. He just stood there, his hands shaking, his chest aching, and tried to understand how something like this had happened inside the compound. Inside the wards. Inside the one place that was supposed to be safe.

The pack was in lockdown. No one in. No one out. The wards were reinforced. The guards doubled. But the question hung in the air like smoke. How did this happen?

Someone opened the wards. Someone invited the Hollow King in.

And Victor couldn't figure out who.

---

AMELIA

She found him in the healing tent, standing over Kael's body, staring at something only he could see. He hadn't moved. He hadn't blinked. He was just standing there, his hands clenched into fists at his sides, his shoulders tense like he was holding up the entire world.

She didn't ask what happened. She didn't try to understand. She just walked over and sat down beside him.

Not touching. Not yet. Just there.

Victor didn't say anything. She didn't say anything. They sat in silence for a long time. The healers worked around them. Emila moved quietly, her face drawn, her hands steady. Josiah stood by the door, his eyes closed, his expression unreadable.

Then Victor spoke. His voice was low, broken, barely a whisper.

"He was my friend," he said. "I trained with him. Since we were pups. I had to put him down. I had to."

And Amelia, she didn't say anything. She didn't offer platitudes. She didn't tell him it wasn't his fault. She just reached for his hand. And when he let her hold it, she held on.

Not tightly. Not desperately. Just firmly. Steadily. The way you hold something you don't want to lose.

And for the first time, Victor let someone see him break.
