# Chapter 13: After the Moon

Victor

He should have gone straight to his quarters. He should have dismounted, locked the door, and slept the way men slept when their bodies had been pushed past endurance. That was the logical thing. That was what he would have done before the full moon. Before the clearing. Before Aurela's voice cracked open his head like a seam and poured centuries of war through it.

He didn't go to his quarters.

He went to the edge of the compound and sat on his horse behind the healing tent, and stared at the tree line until his horse stamped and snorted and he finally let Perseus lead him inside.

Perseus said nothing. He never did, not when Victor didn't want to talk. But he didn't let go of the reins until Victor was off the horse and on his feet, and he didn't let go of Victor's shoulder until they were standing in the doorway of the main hall, and he looked at Victor's face with those blue-gray eyes that saw everything and never flinched, and then he stopped.

"Alpha."

Victor nodded once.

Perseus held his gaze for three seconds. Four. Five. "You didn't sleep."

"I went on patrol."

"You rode all night." Perseus's voice was flat. Not accusing. Not questioning. Just stating the thing that was sitting in the room between them. "That's not patrol."

Victor didn't answer. He walked past him. Perseus fell into step beside him anyway.

"I need you to understand something," Perseus said quietly. "I'm not your mother. I'm not going to ask what happened. But you're walking like you're carrying the weight of the world, and I can read you well enough to know that whatever it is, it's not just a long night."

Victor kept walking. "Perseus."

"Fine. It's your face, not mine. But don't pretend you haven't been affected." Perseus paused at a doorway. "Whatever happened out there tonight, it changed something. And when it does, the pack needs to know. Even if the answer is 'I don't know yet.'"

Victor stopped. He turned his head. "I'll tell you when I know."

"Fair enough." Perseus stepped back. "Get some rest, Alpha."

Victor walked away. He didn't rest.

---

He found himself in the healing tent before he remembered deciding to go there.

Emila was bent over a table, grinding herbs between a mortar and a pestle, her sleeves rolled to the elbows, her dark hair pulled up in a messy twist that kept escaping. She looked up when he entered, and her expression shifted in the space of a heartbeat — warm, then concerned, then something steadier.

She set the pestle down. "Victor."

He stood in the doorway for a long moment. The tent smelled like dried lavender and crushed willow bark and the sharp metallic tang of something he couldn't name — something that reminded him, briefly and unfairly, of the forest, of the clearing, of the way the air had felt when Aurela's voice hit.

"I didn't mean to bother you."

"You're not bothering me." Emila wiped her hands on a cloth and walked around the table. She was shorter than him by a head, but she didn't look up at him with anything less than the same respect she'd show Josiah or Perseus or anyone else in this room. That was why everyone came to her. "Sit down."

He sat.

She didn't sit beside him. She sat across from him, knees touching the table, chin resting in her palm, eyes steady. "You've been walking around the compound like a ghost for two days. I noticed after your patrol this morning. I didn't connect it to tonight until now. But I see you. You're not okay."

"I'm fine."

She held his gaze. "Victor."

"I'm fine."

"You're not." Her voice was gentle, but it didn't soften at the edges. "You're sitting here telling me you're fine, which means you're not. I know the difference."

Victor looked down at his hands. They were resting on his knees, palms open, fingers slightly spread. They were still trembling. Not visibly — not enough for anyone else to see — but enough for him. The same tremor he'd felt in the clearing when the bond hit him like a physical thing, like a hook in his chest, like his body was trying to tear itself apart to get to her. To her. The word felt like a stone in his mouth. He didn't understand it. He couldn't understand it. He had spent his entire life learning to control everything in his power, and this — this pull, this certainty, this *thing* — was the one thing he could not control, and that terrified him more than anything the Hollow King could throw at them.

"It's the bond," he said finally. The word felt foreign. The way Josiah had spoken it had made it sound like a law of nature, like gravity or seasons or the rising sun. But saying it out loud, now, made it feel like something heavy and alive, sitting between them in the tent. "It changed."

Emila didn't blink. "Changed how?"

"It's louder." The tremor in his hands worsened, and he pressed his palms flat against his knees to steady them. "Before, it was a hum. A steady hum. Like standing next to a fire you can feel but can't touch. Now it's... it's a current. It's everywhere. In my hands. In my chest. In my—" He stopped. The image of the clearing flashed through his head unbidden. Her face. Her eyes. The way she'd stood there, trembling, tears on her cheeks, looking at him like he was the only thing in the universe that mattered. He could still feel it. The pull. The certainty. The way his heart had slammed against his ribs like it wanted out.

Emila was quiet for a long moment. When she spoke, her voice was softer. "You felt it too, didn't you? Last night."

He nodded.

"It got louder. For both of us," he said.

"It was like a struck match. It caught fire. I couldn't— I couldn't breathe. I couldn't think. I could only feel her." He looked at Emila. His face was carefully neutral, but his jaw was tight, and his eyes were dark and unblinking. "I've never felt anything like it. Not in my life. Not in the pack. Not in my own body."

Emila's fingers curled around the edge of the table. "And now?"

"Now it's still there." He let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding. "It's not gone. It never went away. It's just... settled. Like a fire that's burning steady after the explosion. It doesn't stop. It doesn't hurt anymore. But it's loud. And I can't shut it off."

Emila nodded slowly. She didn't look surprised. She looked like she'd been expecting this. "Victor."

"Yeah."

"The bond is supposed to work like that. It's not a switch. It's not something you turn on and then turn off." She leaned forward, elbows on the table, voice low. "It's a connection. A real one. And once it's made, it doesn't just disappear because you're uncomfortable with it."

"I'm not uncomfortable." He lied. The words came out flat, almost angry. "I'm... processing."

"Sure." Emila didn't push. She didn't have to. She was one of the few people in the pack who could sit across from an Alpha and not flinch. She'd earned that. "Okay. So you're processing."

"I need to understand what this means." He straightened in his seat, hands still trembling slightly. "Josiah told us about the Mate Bond — Aurela's strategy, the coalition, the Hollow King. I understand the strategy. I don't understand the bond. I don't understand why it feels like— like something inside me woke up and decided to stay awake. I don't understand why I can't stop thinking about her."

Emila's expression softened. "Because she's your mate. Not because of the strategy. Because of her."

"That doesn't help."

"It might not help right now. But it's the truth." She sat back. "You're carrying this like it's a weight. Like you have to figure it all out on your own. Victor, you're the Alpha. That means you're supposed to carry things. But it doesn't mean you carry them alone."

He looked at her. "I don't know what you're talking about."

"You heard her." Her voice was quiet. "Aurela. In the clearing. I was there. I saw it."

He stared at her. "You were in the clearing?"

"I was on the perimeter. I felt something shift before I saw it. I ran as fast as I could. I got there just as the vision started." Her voice was steady, but there was something in her eyes — something he couldn't name. Awe? Fear? Both? "I saw her. Aurela. I felt her. And I saw what happened between you two. It was... it was beautiful. And terrifying. And I've been a pack member long enough to know when something important is happening."

Victor didn't speak. He couldn't.

Emila leaned forward again. "You don't have to carry this by yourself. Not anymore. Not now that you know she feels it too. That changes everything."

"I don't know what it changes."

"Then stop trying to know." She smiled, small and sad and knowing. "Just be honest with yourself. That's all I'm asking."

She stood up, walked around the table, and placed her hand on his shoulder. Not a healer's touch. Not a pack member's gesture. A friend's hand, warm and steady, on a man who didn't know how to let anyone touch him without purpose.

"You're carrying it alone," she said quietly. "You don't have to."

Then she walked away, and he sat there in the healing tent, alone again, listening to the quiet breathing of the pack members resting in the cots, feeling the bond hum through his chest like a second heartbeat, and wondering — for the first time in his life — whether being strong meant holding everything inside or letting someone else see the cracks.

---

Amelia

She couldn't stop looking at her hands.

They were sitting on her kitchen table, palms up, fingers slightly spread, trembling in a way that made no sense. They had stopped vibrating after she'd left the clearing. Stopped aching. Stopped burning. But they were still tingling. A low, steady hum, like electricity running just under the surface of her skin. She pressed her thumb against her index finger and couldn't feel the pressure. Not fully. Just a muffled version of it, like she was touching the world through a sheet of glass.

She was a healer. She knew what her body felt like when something was wrong. She knew what numbness felt like. She knew what nerve damage felt like. She knew what the aftermath of fever felt like. This was none of those things.

This was something else entirely.

She flexed her fingers. They moved, but they didn't feel like her fingers. Not exactly. They felt like his fingers. The thought hit her like a stone in the chest, and she pressed her palms flat against the table to steady herself.

It was ridiculous. It was impossible. It was the kind of thing that made no sense at all.

And it was exactly what had happened in the clearing.

She closed her eyes. She saw the moon. She saw the clearing. She saw him standing on the other side, hair wild in the wind, gray eyes locked on hers, breathing hard, looking at her like she was the only thing in the universe that mattered.

She opened her eyes. Her hands were still trembling.

She stood up and walked to the window. The night was dark, the sky full of stars, the trees at the edge of the property swaying in the wind. Everything looked normal. Everything looked the same as it had this morning, this afternoon, last night, last week, last year. But it didn't feel the same. It couldn't feel the same. Something had changed. Something had shifted. Something had been revealed, and she was standing here in her kitchen, staring at her hands, trying to rationalize a feeling that made no rational sense.

*You look at each other and think it is romance.*

Aurela's voice, echoing in her head, clear and ancient and sad, like a woman who had been carrying a weight she could no longer bear.

*I look at you and see the only thing that will keep the dark at bay.*

Amelia pressed her forehead against the cold glass of the window and closed her eyes.

*Forgive me for using the heart as a weapon.*

Her hands were pressed flat against the window. Her eyes were closed. She was standing in her kitchen at two in the morning, alone, trembling, trying to figure out why her chest ached with the memory of a man she'd met five days ago.

Five days.

She'd never met anyone in five days who made her feel like this. Like her body had been waiting for him without telling her. Like her bones had been searching for him without knowing his name. Like something inside her had woken up and decided it wasn't going back to sleep.

She pressed her palm harder against the glass. The cold seeped through her skin. It didn't help.

She could feel him.

Not his thoughts. Not his voice. Not anything she could name. Just... him. His presence. Like he was sitting in the room with her, even though she was alone. Like he was standing on the other side of the wall, breathing, moving, living, and she could feel every heartbeat, every breath, every shift of weight, every flicker of emotion, like a radio tuned to a station she couldn't control, broadcasting on a frequency she didn't understand but couldn't turn off.

It was overwhelming.

It was terrifying.

It was the most real thing she had ever felt.

She pulled her hand away from the glass and turned around. The kitchen was small. One table. Four chairs. A stove. A fridge. A shelf with jars of dried herbs and a photograph of her grandmother, smiling, her arm around Amelia's shoulders, the crescent moon tattoo visible on her wrist.

She looked at the photograph for a long moment. Then she looked at her hands again.

Her grandmother had been a healer too. Not like this. Not... whatever this was. But she'd known things. She'd known people. She'd known the woods better than anyone in town. She'd always said that the land had a heartbeat, that everything was connected, that nothing was as separate as it seemed. Amelia had listened, nodded, and not entirely believed her.

Now she wasn't so sure.

She walked back to the window. She looked out at the trees. She closed her eyes. She breathed.

The tingling in her hands didn't stop. The pull in her chest didn't stop. The presence in the room didn't stop.

She was going to have to sleep eventually. She knew that. Her body was exhausted. Her mind was fried. Her heart was aching. But she couldn't sleep right now. Not like this. Not with him out there, feeling the same thing, and her unable to do anything about it but stand in her kitchen and stare at her hands and try to figure out why her bones felt like they were reaching for him.

She pressed her palm against the glass again.

The tingling in her hand got slightly stronger.

She didn't know why. She didn't know if it was him. She didn't know if it was the bond. She didn't know if it was anything at all.

But she knew one thing.

She wasn't going to sleep again until she saw him tomorrow.

It wasn't a choice. It wasn't a decision. It was a certainty, like gravity, like the rising sun, like the pull of something she couldn't name and couldn't control and desperately, desperately didn't want to let go of.

The window was quiet. The kitchen was quiet. The night was quiet.

And between her and him, the bond hummed, warm and steady and undeniable.

It was enough. Not for forever. Not for all the years ahead. But for this moment. For this kitchen. For this night. For this quiet.

It was enough.
