Chapter 23
The Full Moon

AMELIA

She felt it at dawn, the same pull she'd felt before, but stronger. The thread between them was taut, vibrating like a plucked string. She knew what it meant before she even thought about it: the moon was coming.

She didn't fight it. She'd learned that lesson already. Instead, she made tea, fed Luna, and sat by the window of her guest room until the sun had climbed high enough to make the walk feel manageable. Her pulse kept spiking without reason, her body humming with anticipation she couldn't name. She waited until mid-morning before she finally stood, pulled on her coat, and left the compound by the eastern gate. The forest grew darker as she got closer, the air thick with that low vibration she'd learned to recognize. Her hands tingled before she even saw the trees.

When she arrived, he was already there.

Victor stood at the center of the clearing, his back to her, his head tilted toward the sky. The moonlight caught the edge of his profile, and for a moment she just watched him, the way his shoulders were tense, the way his hands hung at his sides, the way he looked like a man carrying the weight of the world on his back.

He turned when she stepped into the clearing. His eyes were gray, darker than usual, blown wide with something she couldn't name. And the thread between them pulled so tight it ached.

She didn't say anything. He didn't either. They just stood there, twenty feet apart, the moonlight between them, the bond humming so loud she could hear it in her teeth. Her skin felt too tight. Her breath came faster. She had to force herself to stay where she was.

Then he spoke. His voice was low, rough around the edges. "I didn't think I'd see you."

She stepped closer. "You called me here."

He looked down. "I didn't call you. The bond did."

"That's the same thing."

He didn't argue. He just stood there, his hands in his pockets, his shoulders stiff, his gaze fixed on the ground. She noticed that he was shaking, just slightly, barely visible, but she noticed. The sight of it sent something hot through her chest.

"You're scared," she said.

His jaw tightened. "I'm the Alpha. I don't get to be scared."

"That doesn't mean you aren't."

He looked at her then, really looked at her, and something in his face cracked. Just a little. But it was enough.

"I'm scared of failing them," he said. "Of failing the pack. Of failing you. Of failing Reid."

The name hung between them like a stone. Reid was gone. He'd been put down. The pack was burying him at dawn tomorrow. The pack knew. Everyone knew. But saying it out loud made it real.

"I know," she said.

They stood in silence for a long time. The bond hummed between them, steady and warm, but it wasn't just warmth anymore, it was hunger. A pull she couldn't explain. She wanted to reach out. She wanted to touch his hand, his arm, his shoulder. She wanted to feel him close. She took a step forward. Then another. Until she was standing right in front of him, close enough to see the pulse jumping at his throat.

He didn't move. He just stood there, his hands still in his pockets, his breathing shallow, his eyes locked on hers.

She reached up and touched his face. Her fingers found his jaw, warm and solid under her palm. He closed his eyes for half a second, like he was fighting something, like every instinct in him was screaming at him to do something, anything, and he was holding himself back by sheer force of will.

Then he moved. He pulled his hands from his pockets and held them out, palms up, open. An invitation.

She took them.

The contact was immediate, warmth flooding through her palms, up her arms, into her chest. She felt his heartbeat through his skin, fast, unsteady, controlled but barely. She felt the pulse at his wrists, the rough texture of his skin, the warmth of his hands enveloping hers. She didn't try to feel anything else. She didn't push. She just held his hands and let the warmth do what it could.

But she didn't let go. She held on, and she watched him watching her, and she saw something in his eyes that made her breath catch, something dark and desperate and hungry that she knew was reflected in her own gaze.

He looked down at their hands. His thumb moved, just once, brushing against her knuckles. Then he looked up at her.

"I don't know if I can do this," he said. His voice was lower now, rougher. "Being an Alpha. Being your mate. Being both at the same time."

"Then don't do it alone."

He stepped closer, still holding her hands. One step. Until she could feel the heat radiating off his body, until the space between them was nothing at all. His hands tightened on hers, just slightly, like he was fighting the urge to move them higher, to pull her against him. But he didn't. He just held on, the bond between them tightening like a wire drawn taut.

She tilted her face up. He didn't hesitate. He didn't think about it. He just leaned down and captured her mouth with his, and the kiss was everything she'd been waiting for and more, warm and sure and desperate, his hands finding her waist, pulling her closer, her fingers curling into his shirt. The bond flared between them, not opening, not fully, but widening, the thread stretching tight, and she could feel his heartbeat through his chest against hers, steady and fast and entirely his.

When they broke apart, it was only because she needed to breathe. His forehead rested against hers, his hands still on her waist, his breath warm against her skin. He didn't pull away. Neither did she.

"I want you," he said, and his voice was low and quiet and entirely honest. "I want you close. I want you here. But I won't cross a line you haven't let me cross."

"I know," she whispered.

He didn't move. He just stood there, his hands on her waist, his breath on her lips, his eyes locked on hers, and she could feel the restraint in him like a physical thing, a wire holding back something wild and powerful and entirely his.

They stood like that for a long time. Then he pulled back, slowly, reluctantly. He pressed one last kiss to her temple, his palm resting flat against her shoulder for a heartbeat, before he let his hand fall.

They walked back together, not holding hands, but close enough that the thread between them pulled taut with every step. She could feel the ghost of his touch on her waist, the warmth of his mouth on hers, the way his breath had felt against her lips. She didn't look back at the clearing. He didn't either. They just walked, side by side, his arm brushing against hers with every step, the bond between them warm and steady, growing stronger.

VICTOR

He had been waiting at the clearing for an hour before she arrived. He hadn't planned to wait. He hadn't meant to. He just stood there, pacing, his hands in his pockets, his mind racing through the same loop of worst-case scenarios he'd been running since Reid died.

The bond had pulled him here, not forcefully, not like the first time, but gently, like a current carrying him toward something he knew he needed but wasn't sure he was ready for. The full moon was rising. The clearing was where they'd stood the first time. The place where Aurela had spoken. The place where everything changed.

When Amelia appeared at the edge of the clearing, he felt it — the thread tightening, the warmth spreading through his chest, the pull that he had stopped fighting a long time ago. He turned. He couldn't not turn. She was there. And the bond between them hummed like a live wire.

He didn't know how to start. He never knew how to start with her. She saw too much. She asked the right questions. And she didn't flinch when he gave her the wrong answers.

He looked at her and thought about Reid. His brother. His best friend. The one person who knew what it was like to grow up in his shadow, who knew what it was like to carry that weight and pretend it didn't crush you. Reid had died hollow, and he'd been the one to end him. His oldest friend. His littermate. He'd held Reid's body while it went cold.

That knowledge sat in his chest like a stone.

When Amelia stepped closer, he could feel the heat of her body from ten feet away. She kept coming. He wanted to stop her. He wanted her to keep going. He wanted everything and nothing and both at the same time.

He reached for her, his hands still trembling. She didn't notice. Or if she did, she didn't say anything. She just took them, and the warmth was immediate, flooding through his palms, up his arms, into his chest. He felt her pulse. He felt the steady rhythm of her heartbeat through his skin. He didn't try to feel anything else. He didn't push. He just held her hands and let it be enough.

But it wasn't enough. It was never enough.

She didn't let go. She held on, and he watched her watching him, and he saw something in her eyes that made something primal and desperate rise inside him — hunger, need, a want so sharp it was almost physical.

He looked down at their hands. He didn't want to lose this, not the contact, not the warmth, not the certainty that for this one moment, he wasn't alone. He squeezed her hands. Not tightly. Not desperately. Just firmly. Steadily.

Then she stepped closer, still holding her hands. One step. Until the space between them was nothing at all. He could feel the heat of her body, smell the scent of her skin, see the pulse jumping at her throat. His hands tightened on hers, just slightly, like he was fighting the urge to move them higher, to pull her against him. But he didn't. He just held on, the bond between them tightening like a wire drawn taut.

And then he kissed her.

He didn't plan it. He didn't think about it. He just leaned down and captured her mouth with his, and she was warm and soft and real and she tasted like tea and something uniquely her, and he pulled her closer, his hands finding her waist, her fingers curling into his shirt, the bond flaring between them, not opening, not fully, but widening, the thread stretching taut, and he could feel her heartbeat against his chest, fast and steady and entirely hers.

When they broke apart, it was only because he needed to breathe. His forehead rested against hers, his hands still on her waist, his breath warm against her skin. He didn't pull away. Neither did she.

He could feel her pulse under his fingertips where his hands rested on her waist. He could feel the heat of her skin against his chest. He could feel the way she leaned into him without thinking about it, like she belonged there, like she was meant to be there, and it nearly broke him.

"I want you," he said, and his voice was low and quiet and entirely honest. "I want you close. I want you here. But I won't cross a line you haven't let me cross."

"I know," she whispered.

He didn't move. He just stood there, his hands on her waist, his breath on her lips, his eyes locked on hers, and he could feel the restraint in him like a physical thing, a wire holding back something wild and powerful and entirely his.

They stood like that for a long time. Then he pulled back, slowly, reluctantly. He pressed one last kiss to her temple, his palm resting flat against her shoulder for a heartbeat, before he let his hand fall.

They walked back together, not holding hands, but close enough that the thread between them pulled taut with every step. He could feel the ghost of her mouth on his, the warmth of her body against his arm, the way she leaned into him with every step. He didn't look back at the clearing. She didn't either. They just walked, side by side, with the thread pulling taut between them with every step, and he felt something he hadn't felt in a long time.

Not relief. Not yet. But hope. Small. Fragile. Real.
