Chapter 23
The Full Moon

AMELIA

She felt it at dawn, the same pull she'd felt before, but stronger. The thread between them was taut, vibrating like a plucked string. She knew what it meant before she even thought about it: the moon was coming.

She didn't fight it. She'd learned that lesson already. Instead, she made tea, fed Luna, and sat on her porch until the sun climbed high enough to make the walk feel manageable.

She didn't rush. She walked at her own pace, through the familiar path she'd taken a dozen times before, toward the old clearing near the northern boundary. The forest grew darker as she got closer, the air thick with that low vibration she'd learned to recognize. Her hands tingled before she even saw the trees.

When she arrived, he was already there.

Victor stood at the center of the clearing, his back to her, his head tilted toward the sky. The moonlight caught the edge of his profile, and for a moment she just watched him, the way his shoulders were tense, the way his hands hung at his sides, the way he looked like a man carrying the weight of the world on his back.

He turned when she stepped into the clearing. His eyes were gray. His expression was unreadable. And the thread between them pulled so tight it ached.

She didn't say anything. He didn't either. They just stood there, twenty feet apart, the moonlight between them, the bond humming so loud she could hear it in her teeth.

Then he spoke. His voice was low, barely above a whisper. "I didn't think I'd see you."

She stepped closer. "You called me here."

He looked down. "I didn't call you. The bond did."

"That's the same thing."

He didn't argue. He just stood there, his hands in his pockets, his shoulders stiff, his gaze fixed on the ground. She noticed that he was shaking, just slightly, barely visible, but she noticed.

"You're scared," she said.

His jaw tightened. "I'm the Alpha. I don't get to be scared."

"That doesn't mean you aren't."

He looked at her then, really looked at her, and something in his face cracked. Just a little. But it was enough.

"I'm scared of failing them," he said. "Of failing the pack. Of failing you. Of failing Reid."

The name hung between them like a stone. Reid was still hollowed. The body had been moved to a side room. The pack knew. Everyone knew. But saying it out loud made it real.

"I know," she said.

They stood in silence for a long time. The bond hummed between them, steady and warm. She wanted to reach out. She wanted to touch his hand, his arm, his shoulder. But she didn't. She waited.

He moved first. He pulled his hands from his pockets and held them out, palms up, open. An invitation.

She took them.

The contact was immediate, warmth flooding through her palms, up her arms, into her chest. She felt his heartbeat through his skin. She felt the pulse at his wrists, steady but quick, like a man trying to control something he couldn't control. She didn't try to feel anything else. She didn't push. She just held his hands and let the warmth do what it could.

He looked down at their hands. His thumb moved, just once, brushing against her knuckles. Then he looked up at her.

"I don't know if I can do this," he said. "Being an Alpha. Being your mate. Being both at the same time."

"Then don't do it alone."

He didn't say anything. He just squeezed her hands. Not tightly. Not desperately. Just firmly. Steadily. The way you hold something you don't want to lose.

They stood there until the moon was high overhead, until the clearing felt like the only place in the world. Then he let go, but not before pressing something into her palm, a small, smooth stone he'd picked up from the ground.

"Keep it," he said. "So you know I'm thinking about you."

She closed her fingers around it. "I'll keep it."

They walked back together, not holding hands, but close enough that the thread between them pulled taut with every step. She didn't look back at the clearing. He didn't either. They just walked, side by side, in silence, with the bond between them, warm, steady, growing stronger.

VICTOR

He had been waiting at the clearing for an hour before she arrived. He hadn't planned to wait. He hadn't meant to. He just stood there, pacing, his hands in his pockets, his mind racing with every possible way this could go wrong.

The bond had pulled him here, not forcefully, not like the first time, but gently, like a current carrying him toward something he knew he needed but wasn't sure he was ready for. The full moon was rising. The clearing was where they'd stood the first time. The place where Aurela had spoken. The place where everything changed.

When Amelia appeared at the edge of the clearing, he felt it, the thread tightening, the warmth spreading through his chest, the pull that he had stopped fighting a long time ago. He turned. He couldn't not turn. She was there. And the bond between them hummed like a live wire.

He didn't know how to start. He never knew how to start with her. She saw too much. She asked the right questions. And she didn't flinch when he gave her the wrong answers.

So he didn't answer. He just stood there, shaking, carrying the weight of a hollowed friend and a pack that looked to him for answers he didn't have.

And then she said the one thing he hadn't expected: "I know."

She didn't try to fix it. She didn't offer comfort. She just acknowledged it. That was enough.

When he reached for her, his hands were still trembling. She didn't notice. Or if she did, she didn't say anything. She just took them, and the warmth was immediate, flood through his palms, up his arms, into her chest. He felt her pulse. He felt the steady rhythm of her heartbeat through his skin. He didn't try to feel anything else. He didn't push. He just held her hands and let it be enough.

He looked down at their hands. He didn't want to lose this, not the contact, not the warmth, not the certainty that for this one moment, he wasn't alone. He squeezed her hands. Not tightly. Not desperately. Just firmly. Steadily.

And when she walked back with him, side by side, in silence, with the thread pulling taut between them with every step, he felt something he hadn't felt in a long time.

Not relief. Not yet. But hope. Small. Fragile. Real.
