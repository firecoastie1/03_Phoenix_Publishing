# Chapter 17: The Invitation

Victor

The dream came without warning.

He did not sleep deeply after the pack meeting. His body was exhausted from the hollowed, from the hollowed warrior's grinding, from the hollowed eyes staring at nothing. But his mind would not rest. It kept circling the territory map, the gaps between boundaries, the thin places where the Veil had worn through. He could still feel Amelia's pulse under his fingers. Steady. Steady. Steady.

Then the room changed.

The fire in the hearth went dark. The walls of his quarters dissolved into silver mist. He was standing in a clearing he did not recognize, under a sky that had no stars but glowed with something like moonlight anyway. The light came from everywhere and nowhere. It did not cast shadows. It made them impossible.

A figure stood at the center.

She was not solid. She was translucent, like moonlight made human. Silver hair, luminous eyes shifting between colors, the kind of beauty that made his chest tighten without permission. He knew her. He did not know how he knew her, but he did. Josiah's voice in his head: *Aurela. The Moon Goddess.*

She looked at him with the kind of attention that made him feel seen in a way that had nothing to do with eyes.

"Victor," she said. Her voice was not loud. It was not soft. It was everywhere, like the sound of wind through ancient trees, like the ocean heard from a great distance. "You have met her."

He felt his jaw clench. "Amelia."

"You met her. You brought her to your pack. You felt what happens when two people recognize each other across a distance too vast to be coincidence." Aurela tilted her head. "You do not yet understand what is happening. I can see that in your eyes. You think it is love. You think it is romance. I tell you it is something older. Something necessary."

Victor crossed his arms. His boots felt solid beneath him. His breathing felt steady. But the mist around him pulsed with something that was not wind. "Explain."

"The Hollow King is not the threat you think he is. He is a symptom. The disease is isolation. The Hollow King feeds on it. When shifters stand alone, when they distrust each other, when they hoard their strength and refuse to extend their bonds beyond their own territory, the Hollow King grows. He grows fat on your fear. He grows strong on your silence."

The light shifted. The clearing changed. Victor saw images without looking: a wolf pack standing alone on a mountain ridge, surrounded by darkness that crept closer and closer. A panther pack in a swamp, watching the water rise. A bear pack in the mountains, their fire dying. A fox pack in the desert, their paths closing in. An eagle pack in the peaks, their vision narrowing. A dragon pack in the volcanic lands, their heat fading.

Each pack was strong on its own. Each was surrounded. Each would fall.

"I cannot fight him," Aurela said. "Not directly. The Veil keeps me from entering the physical world. I can only work through the Veil. The only tool I have left is the Mate Bond. Each bond creates a bridge between two people who have no reason to trust each other. Wolf and human. Panther and wolf. Bear and wolf. The bonds multiply. They create a web. A barrier. A coalition."

The images shifted again. Bonds connecting the packs like threads of light, forming a barrier that pushed the darkness back. The Hollow King recoiling. The barrier holding.

"The first bond is always the hardest," Aurela said. "Because it requires the Alpha to trust something he cannot control. Victor, you are a man who holds everything together with his own hands. You carry your father's legacy like a shield. But the shield is not enough. The old ways are not enough. The Hollow King knows every tradition you have. He does not know this."

She pointed at him. At his chest.

"The Hollow King will come for her. He will come for you. He will test the bond. He will try to make you doubt it. And you must not doubt. Not because I made it so, but because the bond is a choice, and you chose her. Not because of magic. You. That is what makes it strong. That is what makes it a weapon."

The light pulsed. Victor felt his heartbeat rise.

"Other bonds will follow," Aurela said. "Perseus. David. Grayden. Samuel. Raine. Each one will find their mate. Each one will create a bridge. Each one will weaken the Hollow King. You are the first Alpha in centuries who has the chance to unite your pack, not through fear or tradition, but through connection. Do not waste it."

The mist began to close in. Aurela's form dimmed.

"Victor," she said. Her voice was fading. "The Hollow King targets bonds because they create barriers. If you refuse the bond, the barrier weakens. If you choose the bond, the barrier holds. Do not let fear make your decision for you."

The dream broke.

---

Victor woke in his chair.

His head was on the map spread across his desk, his neck ached, his hands were cold. The first thing he thought was: *Aurela lies.*

The second thing he thought was: *I do not believe her. Not all of it.*

The third thing he thought was: *I have to see her. I need to see her face.*

He stood up so fast his chair scraped against the floor. He didn't pick up the scattered papers. He grabbed his jacket, threw it on, and walked out of his quarters. He walked the edge of the territory, past the boundary stones, and the bond hummed between him and Amelia like a wire pulled taut.

She was at her cottage. He stopped outside her door. He did not knock. He stood there, listening to the silence, feeling the hum in his chest grow stronger, and he thought: *She is right there. I could go in. I could talk to her. I could tell her what I saw. I could tell her what she means.*

But he did not knock. He did not go in. He turned and walked away.

He walked back through the trees to the compound. His quarters were cold. The fire in the hearth was dead. He sat on the edge of his bed, pressed his palms flat against his knees, and closed his eyes.

*The first bridge is always the hardest.*

*If you refuse the bond, the barrier weakens.*

*Do not let fear make your decision for you.*

"Shut up," he said. Not to the room. To her. To Aurela. To the voice that sounded like the ocean and the wind and something ancient and calculating.

"You do not get to tell me what to do."

He opened his eyes. The room was exactly as he had left it. No Aurela. No mist. No light. Just his quarters, his bed, the cold, and the bone-deep ache of a bond he had not asked for and could not deny.

He threw himself face-down onto the bed.

His hands were shaking.

He had faced warriors twice his size. He had taken blows that would have killed a man. He had shifted in combat with blood in his eyes and his pack's lives hanging on the outcome. But none of that shook him like a dream. None of that made his hands tremble like a goddess telling him his mate was not a love story but a weapon.

He pressed his face into the pillow. He breathed. He counted. He let his breathing slow.

He was Victor Slade. Alpha of the Blackwood Pack. He did not let fear control him. He did not let anyone control him. Not Aurela. Not the Hollow King. Not the Moonlit Rite.

But he also knew, with a certainty that terrified him, that he could not stop thinking about Amelia. He could not stop feeling the hum in his chest when he was apart from her. He could not stop seeing the way she looked at him when she said she wanted truth, not fantasy.

He rolled onto his back. He stared at the ceiling. He let his hands rest flat against his sides. He let his breathing steady.

In the morning, he would go to the war room. He would look at the map. He would make decisions for the pack. He would be the Alpha.

But right now, he was just a man who had been told by a goddess that his mate was a weapon, and he was not sure whether that made him angry or grateful or something he did not have a word for yet.

He closed his eyes. He did not sleep. He thought about her.
