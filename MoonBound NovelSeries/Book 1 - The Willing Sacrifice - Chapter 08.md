# Chapter 8: The Encounter

Amelia woke before the sun.

She slept on her pack — not the most comfortable bed she'd ever had, but it would do. The fire had burned down to embers and the forest was still dark, and the sky still black, the air still cold. She sat up, rubbed her face, and looked at her hands.

They were tingling.

They had been tingling all night. Not strong, not overwhelming — just there, a low vibration like a phone on silent in the next room. She had woken up three times in the night, half-asleep, trying to figure out what was making that noise. It hadn't been the fire. It hadn't been the wind. It had been her hands.

She got up. She brushed off her clothes. She packed the fire and put out the embers with dirt. She ate a piece of dried bread and drank from her water skin. And then she walked into the forest.

---

The forest was different at dawn.

The mist had risen from the ground, low and gray, curling between the trees like smoke. The air was damp, cool, smelling of pine and damp earth and the faint metallic tang of something she couldn't name. The birds had started singing — a few of them, cautious, testing the morning like it was something fragile.

She followed the sound of water.

She hadn't planned to go far. She had planned to walk a short distance, look around, take notes, and come back. But the deeper she went, the more the forest opened up. The trees thinned, the canopy broke, and she could see a stream ahead, narrow and shallow, winding through the rocks and moss like a silver ribbon.

She sat down beside it. She pulled out her water skin, filled it, and drank.

And then she heard the snapping of a branch.

She looked up.

He was standing at the edge of the clearing — tall, dark-haired, gray eyes the color of storm clouds, his body built like someone who had spent his entire life training for a war he didn't know was coming. He was wearing a dark jacket, jeans, and boots. He looked like someone who belonged in the forest.

And he was looking at her.

The bond detonated.

She felt it like a physical blow — a surge of electricity that started in her hands and shot up her arms, through her shoulders, into her chest, like gravity had shifted and pulled everything toward him. Her water skin slipped from her fingers and hit the ground. Her hands were vibrating so hard she couldn't feel them. Her heart was hammering against her ribs.

He froze.

She saw it happen — the way his body went rigid, the way his eyes widened, the way his hands clenched at his sides. He looked like someone who had been hit by a truck. He looked like someone who had felt something he couldn't explain.

He was looking at her. She was looking at him. And neither of them knew what to do.

---

It was the longest three seconds of her life.

She could feel it — the pull, the tension, the invisible thread stretching between them, pulling her toward him with a force she had never experienced in her entire existence. It was physical, undeniable, and terrifying. It was like standing at the edge of a cliff and feeling the wind lift your feet off the ground.

He took a step toward her.

She didn't know why. She just knew he couldn't look away.

Then he spoke.

"You're not supposed to be out here," he said. His voice was low, commanding, but there was something in it that surprised even him — uncertainty, a crack in the armor, a moment of doubt that he couldn't hide.

She looked at him. She was measured. She was not cowering. She was not running. She was standing there, looking at this man who had appeared out of the mist like he'd fallen from the sky, and she was trying to figure out who he was.

"Neither are you," she said.

He blinked. He didn't expect that. He hadn't expected anyone to talk back to him like that. He was used to fear. He was used to respect. He was used to obedience. He was not used to being told that he wasn't supposed to be somewhere — not by a woman who looked like she had stepped out of the forest itself, with her hair messy and her clothes dirty and her eyes wide and curious and not afraid at all.

He noticed all of that. He noticed the way her hands were shaking. He noticed the way her chest was rising and falling too fast. He noticed the way she looked at him — not with fear, not with awe, but with something that might have been recognition.

He noticed that she was the most beautiful woman he had ever seen.

He didn't want to notice that. He had other things to worry about. He had a missing hunter to find. He had a pack to protect. He had a boundary to guard.

But he noticed.

---

"What are you doing out here?" he asked. His voice was steadier now. He had pulled himself together. He was the Alpha. He was used to being in control.

"I live near here," she said. "I'm a healer. I was gathering water."

He looked at her. He really looked at her. He was a man who had spent his entire life reading people — their intentions, their fears, their tells. And he read something in her that unsettled him.

She was not afraid.

Not of him. Not of the forest. Not of anything.

He didn't know what to make of that.

"Your hands," he said. "They're shaking."

She looked at her hands. They were vibrating. She had noticed, of course she had noticed, but she hadn't said anything. She didn't want to seem strange. She didn't want to seem afraid.

"It's nothing," she said.

"It's not nothing," he said. He took another step toward her. He couldn't help it. The pull was too strong. The connection was too real. It was like gravity, like magnetism, like the moon pulling the tide. He didn't understand it. But he couldn't fight it.

She looked at him. Her eyes were dark, intelligent, alive. Her hair was messy, her clothes were dirty, her face was smudged with dirt. She was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen.

And she was looking at him like she had been waiting for him.

He didn't know why that thought had crossed his mind. He didn't know why it felt so true.

"Who are you?" he asked.

She didn't answer.

He didn't know if she had forgotten her name, or if she was just deciding whether to trust him, or if she was trying to figure out who he was in return. He waited. He didn't move. He didn't breathe. He just stood there, in the mist, in the forest, in the space between the trees, and he waited for her to speak.

"Amelia," she said.

"Victor," he said.

Her name was Amelia. His name was Victor. And for a moment — just a moment, just a heartbeat — the world felt complete.

Then the mist thickened. The forest went quiet. And neither of them knew what to do next.
