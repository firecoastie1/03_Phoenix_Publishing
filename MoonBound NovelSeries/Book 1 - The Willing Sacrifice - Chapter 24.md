Chapter 24
The Traitor Revealed

PERSEUS

The reports didn't add up. He'd been going over them for three days, and they still didn't add up.

On paper, the ward breach looked like sabotage. The outer ward had been disengaged from the inside. Someone knew the sequence. Someone had the knowledge. That meant betrayal. It had to. The ward protocols weren't public. They were Alpha-level. Only the inner council knew them.

But Victor was Alpha. Victor gave the orders. Victor was the only one who could authorize the sequence. Unless someone had copied it. Or someone had learned it from him.

Perseus had ruled out copying. The ward logs showed no unauthorized access. The sequence had been executed by someone standing at the control node inside the compound. That meant they were physically present. Inside the walls. At the moment of breach.

He'd interviewed every member of the inner council. He'd checked the logs. He'd walked the perimeter himself. He'd done everything a good second-in-command could do, and every answer pointed to betrayal.

He wasn't ready to accept that. Not yet.

So he went back to the evidence. The physical evidence. The thing he hadn't looked at closely enough.

The control node was a stone pedestal near the healing tent. It was where ward adjustments were made. Routine maintenance, seasonal recalibrations, emergency overrides. The Hollow King attack had happened when someone had overridden the outer ward. They'd disengaged it, let something through, and then let the ward snap back into place behind it.

Clean. Professional. If you knew what you were doing.

Perseus knelt in front of the node. He'd walked past it a dozen times since the attack. A dozen times without really looking. But this time he saw it.

He knelt. He pressed his hand against the stone and felt the residual magic. Cold, wrong, like standing next to a body that had just been put down.

There was a trace of something foreign. Not human. Not shifter. Not anything he recognized. It was faint, almost gone, like a scent after the rain had washed it away. But it was there. And it wasn't disengaging magic. Someone had to stand at the node to disengage the ward. This was different. This was the ward being disengaged. From the outside.

His breath caught.

The ward hadn't been sabotaged from the inside. The Hollow King had done it from the outside.

He'd used his Void Power. Not to override the sequence, but to weaken the ward itself, to thin it enough that the Hollow King could push through. No one had to stand at the node. No one had to be present. The breach was impossible. And that was exactly the point.

Perseus stood up. His hands were shaking.

David was right. Perseus had been looking for a traitor inside the compound. He'd spent three days interrogating pack members, watching their faces, looking for guilt. He'd turned the pack against each other. He'd made them suspicious of their own brothers and sisters.

The truth was simpler and more terrifying. The Hollow King had done this alone.

He walked to the war room and found Victor at the head of the table, David across from him with his head in his hands, Josiah watching from the corner.

Perseus put his papers on the table. "I was wrong."

Victor looked up. "About what?"

"The breach. It wasn't sabotage." Perseus met Victor's eyes. "It was the Hollow King. He weakened the ward from the outside. His Void Power. He didn't need a traitor. He didn't need someone inside the walls."

The room went still.

David pushed back from the table. "That's not possible. The wards are Alpha-rated. Nothing gets through them unless someone opens them."

"Not unless the thing on the other side is strong enough to push through," Perseus said. "The Hollow King doesn't break in. He dissolves the lock."

Victor stared at the table. His jaw worked. He looked relieved. Perseus could see it. But the relief was followed by something darker. Horror.

"He can enter minds," Victor said quietly. "He can manipulate fear. He can turn what we're afraid of against us."

Perseus nodded. "That's what this is. He didn't need a traitor because the breach was always going to be impossible. That's how he gets inside. He makes the impossible real."

Josiah closed his eyes. "The old stories said he didn't just eat bodies. He ate certainty. He made people doubt that they could trust their own eyes, their own senses. Once they doubted, the wards meant nothing."

Victor stood up. He walked to the window. He didn't turn around.

"So it wasn't any of you," he said to the room. To Perseus. To David. To Josiah.

"No," Perseus said. "It was never any of us."

Victor was quiet for a long time. Then he said something that surprised them all.

"Thank you."

It was the first time anyone had said that to him since Reid died.

He turned around. His face was pale, but steady. "If the Hollow King can get through the wards without help, that means he can come back. Any time. Anywhere."

David stood. "Then we fortify. We reinforce the wards. We double the patrols."

Perseus shook his head. "Reinforcing won't help. If he can dissolve them from the outside, reinforcing just gives him more material to dissolve. We need to understand what he's capable of. We need to know if his power is infinite or if there's a limit."

Josiah opened his eyes. "There's always a limit. Everything has a limit."

"Then find it," Perseus said. "Before he does."

Victor nodded. He turned back to the window. He looked out over the compound, over the pack, over the lives that depended on him. The weight of it was crushing. But it was real now. Not a monster in the woods. Not a shadow on the horizon. A predator of the mind.

And for the first time, he understood what he was fighting.

AMELIA

Amelia stood in the kitchen, waiting for tea to steep, listening to the sound of voices coming from the war room. She'd been trying to ignore it. The tension. The low arguing. The way the bond hummed with Victor's stress. But she couldn't. Not anymore.

She'd moved into the guest room weeks ago. Since then, every morning she woke up feeling him through the bond. Not his thoughts, not his feelings, just the hum. The low steady vibration of his presence. Like a second heartbeat. She knew when he was awake before she opened her eyes. She knew when he was near by the way the hum got louder.

It was intimate in a way she didn't have a word for. And she didn't need one.

This morning, the hum was sharp. Tense. It made her skin feel too tight. Her pulse spiked when she thought about him carrying whatever was happening inside those walls.

She poured the tea. She set the mug on the table. She stood there, listening.

The voices stopped.

Then footsteps. Heavy, close. The door opened, and Victor stepped into the kitchen.

He looked wrecked. His hair was messy. His shirt was wrinkled. His face was pale. But his eyes were gray and present and fixed on her like she was the only real thing in the room.

"You're up early," she said.

"Can't sleep," he said. His voice was rough.

She nodded. She knew what that was like. "I was making tea."

He stared at her for a long moment. She could see the weight of it in his shoulders, the way his jaw worked when he didn't want to say what he was thinking.

He moved past her to the counter. He leaned against it, one hand braced on the surface, his body angled toward hers. The space between them closed without either of them moving.

"You're close," she said quietly.

"I didn't realize." He looked down. Looked back up. His gray eyes were dark. "Sorry."

"Don't." She took a half step closer. Close enough to feel the heat of him. Close enough to see the pulse jumping at his throat. "I like it."

His breath caught. Just slightly. But she noticed.

He set both hands on the counter. He didn't look at her. He looked at the wall behind her.

"Perseus just told me something. The breach. It wasn't anyone from the pack. No traitor. No betrayal. It was the Hollow King. He weakened the ward from the outside. His Void Power."

Amelia's breath caught. "That's good, right?"

"Yes." He looked at her. His eyes were darker now. "It's good. It's also terrifying. Because it means he can come back. Any time. Anywhere. He doesn't need anyone to help him."

She set the tea down. She stepped into his space. Fully this time. Until she was close enough to feel the heat radiating off his body. Close enough to smell him. Smoke and mint and something wild underneath.

Her pulse spiked. Her body hummed.

She didn't hide it. He could feel it. The bond carried it.

"Then we don't reinforce them," she said. "We change them."

Victor frowned. "Change them how?"

"Your wards work because they respond to the pack. To the Alphas. To the inner council. They're designed to keep things out. But if the Hollow King can dissolve them from the outside, maybe the answer isn't stronger wards. Maybe the answer is wards that respond differently."

She walked past him to the counter. He followed. Of course he followed. His body moved toward hers like the bond was a current and he was water. She picked up a scrap of paper, found a pen, and started drawing. She didn't know what she was doing. She was winging it. But her hands knew the way.

He was watching her. She could feel it. The weight of his attention. The shape of it on her skin like warmth on her face. She could feel him looking at her hands, her shoulders, the way she held the pen. He wasn't trying to hide it.

"The bond is the strongest thing I've ever felt," she said. She set the pen down. She turned to face him. Her chest was close to his arm. She could feel the stiffness in his muscles, the way he was holding himself still. "It connects us. But it also creates a space. Our space. What if the wards don't just depend on the pack? What if they depend on the bond?"

Victor was quiet for a long time. His hand was still braced on the counter. Her body was between his hand and his body. She could feel the heat of him through her shirt.

His hand moved. Not toward her face. Not toward her waist. Toward her hand. His fingers found hers. Wrapped around them. Warm and solid and exactly what she'd been waiting for.

"Then research," he said. His voice was low. His thumb moved, just once, brushing against her knuckles. "And let me know what you find."

She didn't let go of his hand. She held on. Felt his heartbeat through his skin. Fast. Controlled but barely.

He looked down at their joined hands. Then up at her. His eyes were dark. Hungry. Holding back.

"I need to go," he said. His voice was rougher.

She nodded. She didn't let go right away. She held on for one more heartbeat. Two. Then she let her hand fall.

He turned to leave. He was at the door. He stopped. He didn't turn around.

"I'm glad you're here," he said.

And then he was gone. She stood there, listening to his footsteps fade. Her hand still tingled where he'd held it. The bond hummed between them. Steady. Warm. Pulling.

She picked up her tea. It was cold. She drank it anyway.

---

Later that afternoon, Amelia met Josiah in the garden behind the healing tent. He was sitting on a bench, hands folded, eyes closed. He looked peaceful. He looked old.

She sat beside him. He didn't open his eyes.

"You have a question," he said.

"I have ten thousand," she said.

"Good. That means you're paying attention."

She told him about the wards. She told him about the bond barrier. She told him about Aurela's words. She told him about Perseus's discovery.

Josiah listened without interrupting. When she finished, he opened his eyes.

"You're thinking about this the right way," he said. "The Hollow King is a predator of the mind. He feeds on fear. He creates doubt. And doubt makes the wards weak. That's why he can dissolve them. It's not just power. It's psychological. He makes the wards doubt themselves."

"That doesn't make sense," Amelia said. "Wards don't have minds."

"Then think of them as mirrors," Josiah said. "The wards reflect the pack's certainty. When the pack is united, the wards are strong. When the pack is afraid, the wards are weak. The Hollow King doesn't break the wards. He breaks the pack. And the wards follow."

Amelia absorbed this. It made sense. It made terrible sense. "So the bond could strengthen them?"

"The bond is the strongest form of certainty," Josiah said. "Two people who trust each other completely. That's something the Hollow King can't touch. He can whisper to them, he can prey on their fear, but he can't break what they've chosen. Not if they've chosen it freely."

She looked at the garden. The flowers were blooming. Small white things. She didn't know their names.

"We need to build wards that respond to the bond," she said. "Not just the pack. The bond."

Josiah nodded. "That would require understanding the bond at a deeper level. Not just the physical sensations. Not just warmth and pulse. The emotional structure. What the bond is made of."

"I can learn that," she said.

Josiah smiled. It was the first time she'd seen him smile. It made him look younger.

"You already know more about it than most Alphas," he said. "That's because you're not afraid of it. You're not trying to control it. You're just letting it be."

He stood up. He patted her shoulder. "Keep thinking like this. And you'll be more useful to the pack than you realize."

She watched him walk away. She sat in the garden for a long time after he left. The flowers were still blooming. The hum of the bond was still in her chest. Steady. Warm. Growing.

She didn't know how to build bond-wards. But she knew one thing: she was going to try.
