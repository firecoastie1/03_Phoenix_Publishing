# Chapter 15: The First Attack

Victor

By the time Victor reached the war room, the ten minutes had become two.

Perseus stood over the territory map, one hand braced on the table. David paced along the far wall. A radio sat between them, hissing with a low wash of static.

Samuel was there too. He had come in from the northern patrol, still wearing his field jacket, the cuffs dark with rain.

"What happened?" Victor asked.

"The north watch missed two check-ins," Perseus said. "The search patrol hasn't returned."

Victor's eyes went to the radio. "Any word?"

Samuel's jaw tightened. "They found Marcus at the old marker line."

For a moment the room narrowed to that name.

Marcus had been gone for weeks. No body, no blood, no tracks. Victor had forced himself to treat the silence as a fact, not an answer. Now the name sat between them again, heavier than it had before.

"When?"

"An hour ago. We got one transmission after they found him. No words. Just breathing. Then the radio cut out." Samuel glanced at the set.

David stopped pacing. "We should have gone sooner."

"Then we find them," Victor said. "Samuel, take us to the last marker. Perseus, keep the compound on alert. No one leaves alone."

Perseus folded the map. "I'll send a second team to watch the road."

Victor was already moving.

He detoured through his quarters, unlocked the bottom drawer, and buckled the field knife to his belt before heading to the gate.

They rode north with Samuel in front, David at Victor's shoulder and two warriors behind them. The morning had turned cold. Rain clung to the fir branches and shook loose whenever a horse brushed past. None of them spoke. Victor listened for the forest to give them something: a call, a snapped twig, the sound of another rider trying to get their attention.

The forest gave them nothing.

Rain ticked against the leaves. A crow called once from somewhere to their left, then went quiet. Victor scanned the gaps between the firs, waiting for a shape to break from the dark. His wolf kept pushing against his skin, restless, uncertain where to direct its anger.

He had spent weeks looking for a body. A trail. Anything that would let the pack grieve instead of wonder. Now they had a voice on the radio and a set of tracks that led nowhere. It was worse than silence.

Samuel raised a fist. They stopped at the last marked trail, where a narrow path dropped toward a creek. One of the boundary stones lay on its side in the wet leaves. The cut in the earth beneath it was clean. Someone had moved it carefully.

Victor dismounted. "Tracks?"

Samuel crouched near the stone. "Three sets coming in. Two sets going back."

"Marcus's?"

"I don't know." He looked up. "The ground's been disturbed."

Victor crouched beside him. There were no drops of blood, no broken branches, no scent trail strong enough to follow. The rain should have blurred everything, but the absence felt too clean. It was as if the forest had been wiped down around the marker.

Samuel drew a slow breath through his nose. "I can smell the patrol. Not Marcus."

A voice came from beyond the trees.

"Alpha?"

One of the search patrolmen stepped into view. His face was pale beneath the mud. He held his left arm against his ribs, though there was no blood on his sleeve.

Victor crossed to him. "Where's the rest of your patrol?"

The man swallowed. "He came out of the trees. We thought it was Marcus."

"Was it?"

The patrolman looked past Victor, toward the trail. "It looked like him."

"Did he speak?"

"No. I called him. He didn't answer. He just turned and came at us." The man's fingers tightened against his ribs. "I thought if we got him back to the compound, you could help him."

A branch shifted behind him.

Marcus stepped into the clearing.

His jacket was torn across the chest. Beneath it, the fabric had collapsed inward over a dark hollow in his body. There was no blood on the ground. No torn earth. No sign of a struggle. His eyes were open, fixed on something beyond them all.

Victor knew the crooked line of Marcus's smile, the loud laugh that carried across the hall, the food he kept in his pockets for any stray animal that came near the compound.

This face held none of it.

"Marcus." Victor took one step toward him.

Samuel caught his arm. "Don't."

Marcus's head turned. The movement came a fraction too late, like the body had heard the name and needed time to remember what a name meant.

Then he ran.

David moved first, but Marcus went straight for Victor. There was no warning in his face, no anger, no recognition. Just a body moving with a force that did not belong to it.

Victor caught his wrist. The impact drove him back into the boundary stone. Marcus's other hand closed around his throat.

"Marcus, stop." Victor's voice came out rough beneath the pressure.

No answer. No flicker in those eyes.

Victor's wolf had gone quiet. That frightened him more than the attack. It knew Marcus's scent and voice, had measured the shape of his temper through years of patrols. Now it had nothing to meet across the space between them.

Victor twisted his grip and broke free. David seized Marcus from behind, but the hollowed warrior threw him off hard enough to send him sprawling. Samuel shouted for the others to hold position. The injured patrolman stumbled clear.

The knife rested at Victor's belt. Marcus's hand reached again, fingers rigid, and the empty space where his friend should have been opened inside his chest.

Victor moved inside the next strike and drove the knife beneath Marcus's jaw.

The body went slack.

Victor caught him on the way down. For half a second, he held Marcus against his chest as if that could change what had happened. Then he lowered him to the ground.

Marcus's chest rose once.

Then again.

The patrolman made a small, broken sound behind them.

"Get him back to the compound," Victor said. His hand was slick around the knife. "Keep him restrained. No one approaches alone."

David pushed himself up, one hand pressed to his shoulder. "He's still breathing."

"I can see that."

The words came out too sharply. Victor looked down at Marcus. His eyes remained open. Whatever had made him Marcus was gone, but his body had not understood.

"It's the Hollow King," Samuel said. His voice was low. "He sent him back."

Victor wiped the knife on the wet grass and sheathed it. "Then the Hollow King can come answer for it."

No one argued. They all knew Victor was speaking to the trees because there was no one else there to hear him.

Amelia

Emila was gathering the cups when the pain reached Amelia.

It was a hard pull beneath Amelia's ribs, sharp enough to make her catch the edge of the table. The cups rattled. One tipped, spilling tea across the wood.

Emila turned at once. "Amelia?"

She pressed a hand to her chest. The ache held for one breath, then eased into a tight, unsteady thrum. It wasn't a wound. It wasn't her body. She knew that much.

North.

Toward the compound.

"Victor," she said.

Emila's expression changed. She was already reaching for her coat. "We should go."

Two riders came through the gate, one leading a horse with a man bound across the saddle. A third brought the injured patrolman in behind them. Victor walked beside the bound horse, his jacket dark with rain. There was blood on one hand.

Amelia went straight to him. "Are you hurt?"

"No."

She looked at the blood. "Is it yours?"

"No."

He gave no more explanation. The stillness around him and the ache beneath her ribs filled the silence.

The bound man was taken toward the healing tent. A healer guided the injured patrolman after him, one hand firm at his elbow. David followed, favoring one arm. Samuel stayed by the gate, giving orders to the guards. Everyone moved as if the compound had become a place where sudden sounds could mean danger.

Amelia kept her eyes on Victor. "Who was it?"

"Marcus LaBlanc."

She remembered the name from the search, from the weeks of questions and the empty spaces in every answer. "He was missing."

"He came back." Victor's gaze dropped to his hand. "Not as himself."

Amelia looked toward the healing tent. "Can he hear us?"

"I called his name."

It wasn't an answer. She understood anyway. Her training had taught her how to find breath, a pulse, a response to pain. It hadn't prepared her to look at a living body and wonder if the person inside it was gone.

"I can help Emila check him," she said.

"Not until we know the restraints will hold." His voice was firm, but his eyes stayed on the tent. "Stay with her. Don't go in alone."

The words were an order. The tremor in his fingers made them something else.

"The Hollow King did this?"

"Yes."

Amelia wanted to say something that would make it easier. Nothing came. She had seen grief before. She knew how useless it was to dress it up as comfort.

"I can stay," she said.

His eyes lifted to hers. He looked tired in a way sleep would not fix. "You don't have to."

"I know."

She held out her hand, palm up, and let him decide.

For a moment he only stared at it. Then he set his bloodstained hand in hers, careful not to smear it across her skin. His palm was warm. His pulse beat fast beneath her fingers.

Under their joined hands, the thread drew taut, then settled, warmer than before. It didn't open, and it gave her no thoughts or clear picture of what he had felt in the clearing. His hand stayed in hers. That was enough to tell her he was still here.

Victor closed his eyes once, briefly. When he opened them, his fingers tightened around hers.

"I knew him for years," he said.

"I know."

"I had to stop him."

Amelia didn't tell him it was all right. It wasn't. She stayed beside him and held on.

From somewhere beyond the northern wall, the alarm bell rang.

Once.

Every conversation in the courtyard stopped. Samuel turned toward the tree line. The bound figure in the healing tent began to struggle against his restraints.

Then the bell rang again.
