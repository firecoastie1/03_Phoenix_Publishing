Chapter 20
The Research

AMELIA

The research notes spread across her kitchen table like a puzzle she couldn't stop trying to solve.

She had been reading for three hours. Her coffee had gone cold. Luna was asleep on the counter, tail twitching in her sleep. The morning light had shifted from gold to white, and she still hadn't blinked.

The pattern was there. She could feel it in the lines she'd highlighted, in the marginalia she'd written in her own cramped handwriting, in the way her pulse was picking up even though her brain kept telling her to stay calm, to be methodical, to not jump to conclusions.

She picked up one of the historical records, a scanned page from an archive she'd reached through a mentor in Edinburgh. The text was in Latin, but she'd translated it weeks ago. It described a Mate Bond forming in the Scottish Highlands, 1642. And three months later, the same village was struck — bodies found hollowed, eyes empty, bodies breathing.

She picked up another record. Italian Alps, 1789. Mate Bond formed in a small mountain village. Five months later, the same village burned. All inhabitants gone. No bodies. No trace.

Another. The Black Sea coast, 1812. Mate Bond. One year later, a Hollow King strike that wiped out an entire pack, seventeen shifters, all hollowed, all found lined up in a row like dolls in a display case.

She picked up the last record — not from the archives, but from her own life. The Blackwood Pack. Victor. Herself. Formed. Three months ago. And in the last month, Marcus hollowed. The second hunter. The pack scared to death.

The pattern was clear. Too clear.

Every time a Mate Bond was formed, the Hollow King struck harder. Not the same day. Not the next week. But within months, always within months, the Hollow King came for them.

It wasn't coincidence. It couldn't be.

She sat back in her chair and stared at the wall. Her hands were shaking.

The Hollow King didn't just fear bonds. He hunted them. He used them as a target. Every bond was a flare in the dark, and the Hollow King was drawn to it like a predator drawn to prey.

Amelia closed her eyes and pressed her palms against her eyes. She had brought this on him. She had recognized the bond, and the bond had recognized her, and now the Hollow King was coming for them because of it.

She thought about Victor, standing at the window, looking south, feeling the tension of the pack movement. She thought about how he'd carried the weight of the pack for three years since his father died, alone, never telling anyone what he was carrying. And now he was carrying this too, the bond, the Hollow King, the responsibility, the fear.

She didn't want him to carry it alone. Not anymore.

She packed her notes into a satchel. She put her coffee mug in the sink. She told Luna she'd be back soon. And she walked out the door.

---

VICTOR

The war room was quiet when Amelia walked in.

She didn't knock. She didn't call out. She just pushed the door open and walked in like she had every right to be there, because she did. She had earned the right to be in this room months ago, when she'd looked at him across the clearing and told him she didn't know what was happening to her, but she wasn't going anywhere.

David was there. Perseus was there. Josiah was there. Emila was there. And when Amelia walked in, all of them went quiet.

He noticed that. He noticed the way their eyes shifted toward her, the way the room seemed to rearrange itself around her presence. It was the same reaction he'd seen a hundred times, the pack always noticed when she was near. Not because she was loud or dramatic. Because the bond hummed in his chest whenever she was close, and the pack could feel it.

"You're late," he said.

She smiled, small, amused, just enough to make his chest tighten. "I was researching."

"Research can wait."

"No," she said. "It can't."

And then she put her satchel on the table, pulled out a stack of papers, and started talking.

She laid out the historical records one by one. The Scottish Highlands, 1642. The Italian Alps, 1789. The Black Sea coast, 1812. The Blackwood Pack, three months ago. Each one had a Mate Bond followed by a Hollow King strike. Within months. Always within months.

The pattern was clear.

The war room was silent. David's jaw was tight. Perseus's eyes were narrow. Josiah's expression was unreadable.

And Victor, Victor felt the cold open in his chest, the same cold that had opened when Perseus had questioned him about the bond affecting his judgment. But this was worse. This wasn't doubt. This was confirmation.

The Hollow King wasn't just coming for the pack. He was coming for the bond. He was hunting bonds.

And the bond had found Amelia.

"You're telling me," Victor said, and his voice was low, controlled, but there was something in it, something sharp, something dangerous, "that every time a Mate Bond was formed, the Hollow King struck? That's not coincidence?"

"No," Amelia said. "It's not coincidence."

"And you think he targets bonds specifically?"

"Yes."

"Because they're a threat to him?"

"Yes."

Victor looked at the papers. He looked at Amelia. He looked at the war room, at David, at Perseus, at Josiah, at Emila, and he felt the weight of it all settle on his shoulders like a stone.

He was being used. By the Hollow King, who saw the bond as a weakness to exploit. By Aurela, who saw the bond as a weapon to deploy. There was no neutral ground. There never had been.

He was furious. Not at Amelia. Never at her. He was furious at the situation, at the Hollow King, at the Moon Goddess, at the bond, at the fact that there was no way to protect the pack without putting Amelia in the crosshairs.

"You did the right thing," he said finally. "You found this. You brought it to me."

"I didn't know who else to—"

"You did the right thing," he said again, firmer this time. "Amelia."

She nodded. Her hands were still. Her face was steady. But he could feel the bond hum in his chest, not warmth, not pulse, just the steady presence of her, and the tension in her chest, and the quiet fear she wasn't saying out loud.

He wanted to tell her to stop worrying. He wanted to tell her she was safe. But he couldn't. Because she wasn't safe. None of them were. And saying it would be a lie.

Instead, he said nothing. He just nodded once, and turned back to the map, and tried to figure out what to do next.
