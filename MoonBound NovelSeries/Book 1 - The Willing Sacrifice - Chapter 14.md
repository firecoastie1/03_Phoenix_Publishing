# Chapter 14: The Investigation

Victor

He found Josiah in the archive room before anyone else was awake.

The room was on the east side of the main hall, a long narrow space lined with shelves of leather-bound books and rolled parchments that predated the pack by centuries. Josiah sat at a desk near the far window, a lamp casting amber light over a spread of documents, his reading glasses perched on the bridge of his nose, his hands folded neatly on top of a book the size of a brick.

Victor stopped in the doorway and watched him for a moment. Josiah was a small man, he had never been one for a fight, but he carried himself with the kind of quiet authority that made space around him. He looked up when the floorboards creaked.

"Victor." His voice was warm, as if he had been expecting him. "You're early."

"I didn't sleep."

"I noticed." Josiah closed the book, set his glasses on the desk, and steepled his fingers. "Sit down."

Victor sat in the chair across from him. He did not relax. He never sat and relaxed; his shoulders stayed squared, his hands stayed ready, his eyes stayed scanning. It was not a habit he could break any more than he could break his own bones. But Josiah did not ask him to. He never did.

"It's the bond," Victor said. No preamble. No circling. "I need to understand what I'm dealing with."

Josiah's expression did not change. He had heard it before, the word, the weight behind it. Those who had been old enough to remember the last bonds knew what it meant.

"I wondered when you'd come to me." Josiah leaned back in his chair. "Or to Emila."

"I went to Emila." Victor's jaw tightened. "But I need the history. What she told me was... practical. I need to know why this happened. What it means. How often it happens."

Josiah was quiet for a long moment. He looked at the closed book on his desk, then past it, into the space beyond Victor, into memory, into the centuries that pressed against the edges of his mind.

"It is rare," he said finally. "Not unprecedented. But rare."

Victor nodded once. "How rare?"

Josiah tapped the cover of the book with one finger. "I cannot give you a number. We do not keep records the way you might expect. Mate bonds are not events we log in ledgers. They are... living things. They breathe. They change. They are not things we can contain in ink."

"Then give me what you can."

Josiah opened the book. It was thick, leather-bound, the pages yellowed and brittle at the edges. He turned to a marked page, a ribbon of faded crimson marked the place, and the handwriting on the page was different from the rest: sharper, more deliberate, as if the person who had written it had wanted to make sure it could be found.

"This was written by Elara of the Silver River pack," Josiah said. "She was an Elder three centuries ago. She documented the bonds she witnessed. And she documented the ones she heard about from elders before her."

Victor looked at the page. The handwriting was tight and precise, the language formal. He could not read all of it, the pack's written history went back further than he could trace on his own, but Josiah began to read aloud.

*"The first bond was not born of strategy but of necessity. The old ones say that when the Moon Goddess created us, she created us incomplete, each type carrying a piece of something larger, each type needing the others to stand whole. A wolf without a panther cannot see in the dark. A bear without a fox cannot hide from the void. An eagle without a dragon cannot fly beyond the clouds. The bonds were her way of stitching us together."*

Josiah paused. He looked up at Victor.

*"Bonds are not common because the conditions that create them are not common. The Moon Goddess does not choose lightly. She sees the future the way we see the ground beneath our feet, everything at once, all at once, and she knows what will happen when two hearts align. She chooses not for happiness. She chooses for survival."*

Victor stared at the page. He had heard Aurela's voice in the clearing. *I look at you and see the only thing that will keep the dark at bay.* It had not sounded like a blessing. It had sounded like a burden.

"Why me?" The question came out flat. Direct. He did not sugarcoat it. "Why Amelia? She is human. She is not a shifter. She does not know what she is."

Josiah's expression softened. "She is not a shifter. You are correct about that. But you are wrong about what that means."

He turned the page. Another passage.

*"The human is the bridge. The old ones say that the Void cannot consume what it cannot understand. A shifter bonded to a shifter is strong, but the Hollow King knows the language of shifter magic. It speaks it fluently. It feeds on it. But a human, a human is silent to it. A human is a blank page. The bond between a shifter and a human is the one the Hollow King cannot predict, cannot replicate, cannot devour. That is why the Moon Goddess chooses a human."*

Victor's chest tightened. Not from the bond. From the weight of what Josiah was saying.

"You're saying my mate, a human woman who didn't know about shifters two weeks ago, is the key to defeating the Hollow King."

"I am saying that the Moon Goddess chose her for a reason. Whether you understand that reason or not, she was chosen." Josiah closed the book. "There is more. The bond grows stronger over time. It is not a one-time event. It deepens with intimacy, emotional, physical, all of it. And once it is made, it cannot be broken. You can resist it. You can run from it. But you cannot unmake it. It is part of your body now. Your magic. Your blood."

Victor was quiet. He processed this the way he processed everything: analytically, methodically, like a battlefield map he was memorizing.

"And if I refuse it?"

Josiah did not flinch. "You cannot. But if you try, the bond does not disappear. It festers. It becomes a source of pain, not comfort. The more you resist, the worse it gets. You have seen this. You felt it in the clearing."

Victor had felt it. The way the pull had gone from a hum to a current, from a current to a physical ache in his chest, like his body was trying to tear itself apart to get to her. He had felt it. He could not unfeel it.

"How often does this happen?" he asked. "Between shifters and humans?"

Josiah was quiet. He was choosing his words carefully, and Victor recognized that as a rare thing, since Josiah rarely hesitated before speaking.

"Three times in recorded history," he said at last. "Three times in three hundred years. Each time, the bond was created to strengthen a coalition against the Hollow King. Each time, the bonded pair was tested. Each time, the Hollow King struck harder than ever before. Because the bond is a threat to him. It is a barrier he cannot cross."

Victor looked down at his hands. They were still trembling. Not from fear. From the bond. From the awareness of her, her presence, her pull, the way his body knew hers the way it knew its own breath. He had never wanted anything this badly in his life. And he had never felt more powerless.

"Is there any way to...?" He stopped. He did not know how to finish the sentence. To make it stop? To control it? To make it go away?

No. That was not what he wanted.

He wanted to go to her.

But he was afraid of what that meant.

Josiah saw it on his face. He always did. That was one of the things Josiah had been useful to Cyrus for, the ability to read a man without that man saying a word.

"Victor." Josiah's voice was gentle, but it did not soften at the edges. "You are carrying this like it is a war. It is not a war. It is a choice. You can choose to resist, and you will suffer. Or you can choose to accept it, and you will live. But you cannot stand in the middle. The bond will not let you."

Victor looked up. "You make it sound simple."

"It is not simple." Josiah stood up. He was shorter than Victor by a head, but he did not look up at him with anything less than the same respect. "It is the hardest thing you will ever do. But it is not a war. You do not have to defeat it. You have to live with it. And if you choose to, you might find that it is not the enemy you think it is."

Victor stood up. He nodded once. "Thank you."

"Go to her," Josiah said quietly. "Before the day ends."

Victor did not answer. He turned and walked out of the archive room. He did not look back.

---

Amelia

She was at the clinic by seven.

She had not slept. Her bed had been exactly where it always was, neat, unrumpled, untouched, and she had stood in the middle of the room at three in the morning, staring at it like it had betrayed her, before walking to the kitchen and sitting at the table until the sun came up. Her hands had tingled the entire time. Not a constant hum anymore. A pull. A direction. North. Always north. Toward the compound.

She had not gone there. Not yet. But she had told Perseus she would come back, and Perseus did not ask questions about why she was coming back or what she was going to do. He had simply nodded and said, "I'll tell the Alpha you're here," as if she and Victor were already a thing he could discuss with other people. As if the bond was not still the most terrifying thing she had ever felt. So she had started where she knew how: at the clinic. Getting her hands busy. Pretending that sweeping the floor and boiling water for tea was the same morning routine as always. It wasn't. It couldn't be. Everything felt different now. Everything felt like it had been tilted on an axis she could not see, and nothing was level anymore. She moved through it mechanically: arranging the herbs, boiling the kettle, stacking her stool just so. Her hands moved, but her mind was somewhere else. Somewhere north. She was making tea when the door opened.

Emila walked in, fresh from the compound. She was wearing a blue sweater and sweatpants, her hair in a messy bun, a clipboard in one hand. She paused when she saw Amelia.

"Hey." Emila's voice was warm. "I didn't expect to find you up this early."

"I didn't sleep."

Emila's expression shifted — not concern, exactly, but recognition. She leaned against the doorframe and set the clipboard on the counter. "Rough night?"

"Something like that."

Emila nodded, like she understood without needing details. "I was down on the road — got caught up with some pack business — and I thought I'd stop by on my way back. You look like you could use company."

"Company sounds good." Amelia set the kettle down and pulled out a second cup. "What did you have in mind? Chamomile?"

"Sure." Emila smiled faintly. "Chamomile sounds good."

She poured two cups and set one in front of Emila before sitting down at the small table near the window. She rested her elbows on the table and looked at her hands. They were still tingling. The pull was still there. North. Toward the compound. Toward him. "I don't know," she said honestly. "I don't know how to describe what's happening."

Emila took a sip and set the cup down. "You don't have to describe it. You just have to tell me what you need."

Amelia looked at her. Emila's eyes were warm and steady, the way they always were. She was one of those people who could sit with you in the dark without trying to turn on the light. That was rare.

"I need to understand," Amelia said. "About the pack. About the Veil. About... about the bond." She hesitated. The word felt strange in her mouth now. Not foreign, exactly. Familiar. She had heard it enough times for it to stop feeling alien. But it still carried weight. "Josiah explained the Mate Bond to me. But I need more. I need to know what I'm signing up for."

Emila did not blink. She had been expecting this. Amelia could tell.

"Okay." Emila leaned forward. "What do you want to know?"

"What is the Veil?"

"The Veil is a barrier. Between the shifter world and the human world. It keeps us separate. It has kept us separate for thousands of years. The Moon Goddess created it to protect shifters, to keep humans from discovering us, from hunting us, from fearing us. But it is weakening. That is why you have been seeing things. Strange lights. Shadows. The feeling of being watched. That is the Veil thinning. It is not supposed to happen."

Amelia absorbed this. "And the bond?"

Emila was quiet for a moment. She was choosing her words carefully. Amelia noticed.

The Mate Bond is... it is a connection created by the Moon Goddess. It bonds two people together. Not just emotionally. Physically. Magically. But there is a process to it. Two stages. The first stage is when the bond forms. The Moon Goddess reaches across the distance and links two people. You feel that — hands tingling, chest aching, a pull in a specific direction. You feel the other person's presence as a hum, like static in the air. It is real. It is undeniable. But it is incomplete.

She set the cup down and met Amelia's eyes.

The second stage is when the bond is completed. There is a ceremony — the Moonlit Rite. The shifters and their mates perform it under a full moon. When the ceremony happens, the bond opens fully. You feel everything. Not just the pull. Not just the tingling. You feel what your mate feels. You feel their emotions, their body, their presence across any distance. The bond becomes a bridge. Before the ceremony, it is a thread. After the ceremony, it is an open channel.

She paused. Right now, yours is in the first stage. It is strong, but it is not yet complete. And when you complete it, you will feel something entirely different from what you feel now.

Amelia looked down at her tea. Her hands were warm now. The tingling had settled into a steady hum. A low current running under her skin.

"Is it dangerous?"

Emila did not flinch. "Yes."

Amelia looked up. "How?"

"Not because of the person you are bonded to. Because of what it makes you vulnerable to. The Hollow King targets bonded pairs. If you are bonded to a shifter, especially an Alpha, you become a target. The Hollow King knows the bond makes you stronger, and it knows that makes you a threat. It will try to take you. It will try to separate you. It will try to break the bond."

Amelia's chest tightened. Not from the bond. From the weight of what Emila was saying.

"The Hollow King," she repeated. "I've heard that name. Josiah mentioned it."

Emila nodded. "He is an ancient predator. Older than the Moon Goddess's creations. He feeds on shifter magic. He tears out shifters' hearts and leaves behind hollowed, bodies that are still breathing but stripped of everything that made them who they were. He cannot enter pack territory uninvited. But the Veil is weakening, which means he is getting stronger. And bonded pairs are his primary target."

Amelia was quiet. She processed this the way she processed everything: carefully, methodically, weighing the information against what she already knew.

"So the bond is not just a romantic thing. It is a weapon."

"It is a shield. A weapon. Both. It depends on how you use it." Emila's voice was steady. "The Moon Goddess does not create bonds for fun. She creates them because she has to. Because the Hollow King is a threat, and the bonds are the only thing strong enough to stop him. You are not a bargaining chip, Amelia. You are not a tool. You are a person the Moon Goddess chose because she saw something in you that she needed."

Amelia looked at her hands. They were tingling again. Not from fear. From awareness. From him. She could feel him, even though he was not in the room. She could feel his presence like a hum in the back of her throat, like a note held in the air between them.

"Why me?" The question was quiet. Almost fragile.

Emila did not give her a diplomatic answer. She gave her the truth.

"Because you are not a shifter."

Amelia blinked. "What?"

"The Hollow King cannot consume what it does not understand. A human is silent to him. He has no language for human magic because there is none. The bond between a shifter and a human is the one he cannot predict, cannot replicate, cannot devour. That is why the Moon Goddess chose you. Not despite being human. Because of it."

Amelia stared at her. The information landed like a stone in still water, ripples spreading outward, touching every edge of everything she thought she knew.

"I don't know how to be a weapon," she said quietly.

"You don't have to be." Emila reached across the table and took her hand. Her grip was warm and steady. "You just have to be you. That is enough. That is always enough."

Amelia closed her eyes. Her hands were tingling. The pull was still there. North. Toward the compound. Toward him.

She opened her eyes. "I need to see him."

Emila smiled, small and knowing. "I figured."

---

Victor

He stood outside the clinic for ten minutes before he went in.

The compound was quiet in the morning. The sun was up, but the light was soft, filtered through the trees at the edge of the property, casting long shadows across the ground. He could hear the sounds of the pack waking: boots on gravel, doors opening and closing, voices carrying faintly across the yard. The pack was alive. Breathing. Existing. And he was standing outside a building he had never paid attention to before, wondering what it would be like to walk into a room where a human woman was making tea.

He had spent his entire life preparing for war. He had spent his entire life learning to fight, to lead, to carry the weight of a pack on his shoulders. He had never been taught how to walk into a room where someone he cared about was waiting for him.

He pushed the door open.

Amelia was sitting at a small table by the window. She was facing the door when he walked in, which meant she had been waiting. She looked up when the door opened, and her eyes met his, and the bond between them detonated like a struck match.

She was wearing a sweater the color of dried lavender and jeans. Her hair was tied up in a loose ponytail, a few strands escaping around her face. She looked normal. She looked like the most normal thing he had ever seen in his life.

And she looked at him like he was the only thing in the universe that mattered.

The bond surged between them, a physical pull, like gravity, like a thread tightening between their chests. He felt it in his hands. In his chest. In the space between his ribs, where something was waking up and refusing to go back to sleep.

She stood up.

"Victor." Her voice was steady. Not hesitant. Not afraid. Just his name.

"Amelia." He stepped into the room. The door clicked shut behind him.

They stood across from each other, three paces apart, and the bond hummed between them like a second heartbeat.

"You came in early," she said.

"I didn't sleep."

"Me either."

He looked at her. Really looked at her. Her warm brown eyes. Her dark hair escaping its tie. The way she carried herself, not nervous, not cowering, but present. Anchored. She was a healer. She knew what her body felt like. She knew what pain felt like. She was standing here feeling something she could not name, and she was not running from it.

"How are you?" he asked.

She smiled, small and sad and knowing. "I am tired. I am scared. But I am here."

He stepped closer. Two paces. The bond pulled. He could feel her pulse in his wrists. He could feel her breathing in his lungs. He could feel the warmth of her skin through the space between them.

"I went to Josiah," he said. "He told me about the bond. About the Veil. About the Hollow King."

Amelia nodded. "I talked to Emila."

"What did she tell you?"

"That the bond makes me vulnerable. That the Hollow King targets bonded pairs. That I am a human and that is why the Moon Goddess chose me." Her voice was steady. Measured. "She said I do not have to be a weapon. That I just have to be me."

Victor was quiet. He processed this the way he processed everything: analytically, methodically.

"She is right."

"I know." Amelia's smile was faint. "I know."

He stepped closer. One pace. The bond was so strong it made his chest ache.

"Amelia." He said her name like a prayer. Like a promise. "I do not know how to do this. I do not know how to be what you need. I have spent my entire life learning to fight, not to... to be close to someone. To let someone in."

She looked at him. Her eyes were steady. Not judging. Not pitying. Just seeing him.

"You do not have to know," she said. "You just have to try."

He reached out. His hand trembled. Not from fear. From the bond. From the pull. From the certainty that was deeper than thought, deeper than logic, deeper than anything he had ever felt in his life.

He touched her hand.

The bond responded.

Warmth flooded through him — not the full opening, not yet, but a spark along a thread that had not been lit. Her warmth. Her presence. Solid and real. He could feel her pulse against his fingers, steady and warm, and it grounded him in a way nothing had in a long time. He let her feel him back: the steady rhythm of his heartbeat, the tension in his muscles slowly uncoiling. Nothing deeper than that. Not until the ceremony. But this was enough. This was real. And for the first time, they understood what the bond was for: not magic, not destiny, but this — the ability to reach across the distance and know, for one moment, that the other person was there.

He did not let go.

She did not pull away.

They stood there, across from each other in a small room with a window and shelves of herbs, holding hands, feeling each other through a connection that predated language, and Victor thought, for the first time in his life, that maybe the thing he had been carrying was not a weight. Maybe it was an invitation.

---

The bell above the door rang.

David walked in. He stopped when he saw them.

"Oh. Sorry." David's voice was carefully neutral. He did not look away. He looked directly at Victor. "I was looking for you. Perseus wants you in the war room."

Victor did not let go of Amelia's hand. He did not even look at David.

"Tell Perseus I will be there in ten minutes."

David's expression shifted. Surprise. Amusement. Something like respect.

"Right. Ten minutes." He turned and walked out, closing the door behind him.

Amelia squeezed his hand.

"Ten minutes," she said.

Victor smiled. Small. Faint. But real.

"Ten minutes."
