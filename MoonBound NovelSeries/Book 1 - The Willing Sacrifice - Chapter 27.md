VICTOR

He takes the stairs down slowly. The compound wakes around him — a low murmur of voices, footsteps on wooden floors, the clink of plates and cups. He walks through the main hall toward the war room. He expects the usual morning: Perseus with a stack of reports, David pacing, Emila with a tray of tea, Josiah sitting quietly in the corner with his eyes closed.

But the moment he steps into the corridor, he feels it.

Not a sound. Not a scent. A shift.

It is subtle. Barely noticeable. But it is there — a ripple in the air, a pressure on his skin, like walking into a room where everyone's attention has already turned toward him.

He keeps walking. But the feeling grows. It is like walking into a current. People glance up as he passes. Not stare. Not gawk. But glance. A warrior by the supply room door looks at him and nods. A healer carrying a tray of herbs stops mid-step and watches him pass with a small, puzzled expression. An inner council member — Samir — looks at him with something like surprise.

He reaches the war room. He pushes the door open.

David is there. Already pacing. Already red-faced. Perseus is at the table, head bent over a map. Josiah sits in the corner, eyes closed. Emila is by the stove, stirring a pot of tea.

The moment Victor walks in, David stops. He stares.

"What?" David says.

Victor walks to the head of the table. Sets his hands on the surface. "What is going on?"

David blinks. He looks away. Then back. "Nothing. I was just — the Hanley scouts. Southern border. Perseus thinks they're pushing again."

Perseus looks up. His eyes are sharp, calculating — but there is something else there. Something that Victor cannot place. Curiosity? Concern? Both?

"David is right," Perseus says. "But not about the Hanley scouts." He meets Victor's gaze. "You're different."

The room goes quiet. Josiah opens his eyes. Emila turns from the stove.

"How?" Victor asks.

Victor turns to him.

David nods. Reluctantly. But he nods. "Understood."

Perseus considers this. Then he nods too. "I will watch the southern border. If the Hanley scouts are moving, I will know. But..." He hesitates. "I will also watch you. Not against you. For you. You're not used to this — the bond, the openness. You should have someone watching your back while you adjust."

Then he nods. A slow, reluctant nod. "Understood."
