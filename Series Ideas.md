## Option C: Space Opera / Academy ← STARTING HERE

### Logline
Military academy in space — cadets pilot mechs or starfighters instead of riding dragons. Factions across different planets or species. Energy powers, ancient ruins, alien tech.

### Core Elements
- **Setting:** Military academy orbiting a strategic planet or located at a frontier space station; border planets between inner and outer system
- **Power system:** Three-part rating system — Neural Link percentage (how well cadet bonds with craft/weapon), Military Tactics knowledge (strategic thinking), Physical endurance (stamina). Ratings: F → E → D → C → B → A → S → SS → SSS (SSS super rare). System only measures neural link up to A — higher ranks are too raw for the technology to quantify.
- **Rating:** The MC has high ranking in Military Tactics, near-bottom in Physical endurance, and CANNOT be ranked in Neural Link because their link is so raw it could KILL everyone nearby until they get other ratings up higher.
- **Elimination:** Academy doesn't intentionally kill students. Elimination comes from cadets killing each other during combat training, and some instructors using challenges to eliminate perceived threats. The academy allows this as a teaching philosophy — "in war, you can't let your guard down." Only those who put in real effort survive.
- **Hierarchy:** Top cadets = Wingleaders equivalent with brutal elimination consequences. Those who don't make the cut face consequences appropriate to space (stranded, reassigned to menial postings, branded a failure by family).
- **Neural Link mechanics:** Normal cadets (F through SS) have 50-80% neural link with their craft. Higher rank = faster craft reactions without physical movement. But piloting is PHYSICALLY demanding — without the body to handle the forces from ultra-fast neural reflexes, the effects are dangerous: passing out and mistaking friendlies for foes. The MC's link is FAR beyond 80% — craft reacts INSTANTLY, but body can't handle it. MC needs higher Physical Endurance to survive, and higher Tactics to anticipate threats before pushing the link to the limit.
- **MC's origin:** Border planets — the zone separating inner planets from outer planets. Creates personal stakes on both sides of the civil war.
- **Conflict backdrop:** Civil war between outer planets (fighting an alien threat) and inner planets (who deny the alien threat exists). Unknown to everyone at the academy in Book 1.
- **Romance:** Tessa Voss — inner planet, old military family. Pilots by instinct rather than strategy. Believes strategy only gives a rough plan of attack but instinct gives you the advantage. Her approach directly challenges Silas's worldview — he calculates everything, she trusts her gut. Creates natural tension in every training exercise and simulation.
- **Protagonist:** Silas Thorne — strategic genius from border planets with no physical endurance, an uncontrolled neural link that frightens everyone, and a flaw of calculating everyone as variables. Must learn to compensate using tactics, build physical endurance to survive his own power, and — most importantly — learn to see people as more than tactical pieces.

### Series 1: "The Academy Arc" (3-5 books)
- Book 1: Silas enters the academy, meets Tessa and the rival/love interest. Silas starts the book treating everyone as variables; ends the year seeing people as friends, lovers, enemies — not just tactical pieces. Academy upgrades testing equipment — Silas's real Neural Link rank is revealed as a cliffhanger setting up Book 2.
- Book 2: Year 2 at the academy — higher stakes, higher rankings, the alien threat starts becoming real. Silas confronts instructors who use challenges to eliminate threats. Romance deepens as Silas and Tessa must rely on each other's different approaches to combat.
- Book 3: Major competition/tournament arc — Silas's strategic genius vs. raw power opponents. Romance develops. Political tension between inner/outer planets reaches the academy.
- Book 4: Betrayal — someone close is on the other side. Silas's unverified rank causes a crisis.
- Book 5: Year 3 graduation — Silas deploys, the alien threat becomes undeniable, setup for Series 2.

### Series 2: "The War Arc" (3 books) — *Spinoff*
- Different POV characters — maybe a surviving academy grad, or a ground commander
- Explores the war that was foreshadowed in Series 1
- Introduces alien civilizations, political backstory

### Series 3: "The Origins Arc" (2-3 books) — *Spinoff*
- Prequel exploring how the academy was founded
- First contact with the alien civilization that created the Origin Powers
- The ancient war that the academy was built to prevent

### Market Position
- Between Fourth Wing (dragon academy) and Ender's Game (military academy)
- Sci-fi angle differentiates from the romantasy wave
- Space academy is a growing subgenre with less saturation
- Romance pulls in the same audience but plot-forward keeps it broader

## Option A: Urban Fantasy / Hidden World

### Logline
Modern-day world with a hidden supernatural layer — magic systems, secret societies, ancient beings living among humans. Different factions at odds.

### Core Elements
- **Setting:** Contemporary Earth with hidden supernatural cities, secret societies
- **Power system:** Blood magic, elemental forces, ancient pacts
- **Factions:** Shadowhunters-style multiple groups with different philosophies about how to handle the supernatural world
- **Romance:** Woven across factions — forbidden attraction, political marriages, slow-burn enemies

### Multi-Series Structure
- Series 1: YA protagonist discovers hidden world (The Mortal Instruments model)
- Series 2: Adult POV — different faction, same universe (The Last Hours model)
- Series 3: Prequel — how the factions were founded (The Courtyard model)

## Option B: Sci-Fi / VR Hybrid

### Logline
Near-future world where humanity lives partly in a massive virtual world with its own rules, factions, and supernatural elements that bleed into reality.

### Core Elements
- **Setting:** Dystopian real world + sprawling VR world with its own physics/magic
- **Power system:** Glitches in reality that correspond to supernatural abilities in-world
- **Factions:** Tech corporations vs. independent players vs. those who discovered the "real" source of the VR world
- **Romance:** Two worlds, two relationship dynamics — in-world romance vs. real-world connection

### Multi-Series Structure
- Series 1: Competition/tournament in the VR world (Ready Player One model)
- Series 2: Real-world consequences — the VR world collapsing or being taken over
- Series 3: The architects' story — who built it and why
