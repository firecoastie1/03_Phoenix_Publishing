# Book 1 - Space Academy: Beat Sheet

**Working Title:** *The Academy Arc — Book 1*
**Protagonist:** Silas Thorne — border planet, high Tactics, low Physical Endurance, UNRANKABLE Neural Link
**Love Interest:** Tessa Voss — inner planet, old military family, pilots by instinct
**Core Arc:** Silas learns to see people as more than variables
**Primary Narrative:** Academy competition (training exercises, simulation tournaments, squadron assignments)
**Backdrop:** Civil war between inner/outer planets; alien threat rumors (unknown in Book 1)
**Chapters:** 25
**Act Structure:** Act I (1-8) → Act II (9-20) → Act III (21-25)

---

## ACT I: THE ARRIVAL (Chapters 1–8)

### Chapter 1 — Arrival at Station Helios
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas arrives at Vanguard Academy on Station Helios, a military outpost orbiting a strategic border planet. He's assigned to his first simulation exercise. Meets Tessa Voss as his squadron partner — their immediate clash: he calculates, she trusts instinct.
- **Character (Silas):** Treats Tessa as a "variable" in his assessment. Sees the academy as a system to navigate, not a community to join.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** First meeting — friction. Tessa dismisses his calculations as "overthinking." Silas dismisses her instinct as "unpredictable."
- **Dark/World-building:** Introduces Station Helios — massive space station, inner planet cadets in polished uniforms, border cadets in worn gear. The political divide is immediately visible. Outer planet cadets speak quietly; inner planet cadets speak freely. A rumor about "alien attacks on the outer rim" is mentioned casually by a border cadet and dismissed by an inner planet cadet as "excuses."

### Chapter 2 — Physical Training
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas struggles in Physical Endurance training — ranks near the bottom of the class. In Military Tactics class, he scores highest. The contrast draws attention. Tessa notices his Tactics score and challenges him to a sparring match he cannot win physically.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins to understand that raw ability isn't everything. He's used to winning through strategy, but here strategy needs a body to execute it.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Rivalry forms. Tessa sees his Tactics score as "book learning" — all theory, no gut. Silas sees her instinct-based approach as reckless.
- **Dark/World-building:** Introduces the three-part rating system: Neural Link %, Military Tactics, Physical Endurance. Scale F → E → D → C → B → A → S → SS → SSS. Normal cadets have 50-80% Neural Link. The academy only measures up to A — anything beyond is "unquantifiable."

### Chapter 3 — First Flight Simulation
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas and Tessa are paired in a flight simulation — starfighter combat against AI opponents. Silas's calculations are perfect; Tessa's instinct overrides them at a critical moment and they succeed. The simulation results show two different scores: Silas's Tactics would have won, but Tessa's instinct saved them at the end.
- **Character (Silas):** Experiences his first crack in his worldview. His calculations were correct, but the simulation required something his calculations couldn't predict — improvisation. He doesn't understand this yet, but he notices it.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Grudging respect. Neither admits the other was right. They argue about who "earned" the win, but the argument is less hostile than before.
- **Dark/World-building:** Introduces Neural Link mechanics — how pilots bond with starfighter craft via neural interface. The higher the percentage, the faster the craft reacts to threats. Piloting is physically demanding: the craft reacts faster than the body can process, which is why Neural Link and Physical Endurance are linked.

### Chapter 4 — The Rating Ceremony
- **Beat (Plot):** First official rating ceremony. Cadets receive their initial ratings displayed publicly. Silas's Tactics and Physical scores are posted. His Neural Link test — the system maxes out at A and cannot process his reading. Cadets whisper. Instructors exchange glances.
- **Character (Silas):** Humiliation mixed with curiosity. He realizes the system can't measure him — not because he's weak, but because he's beyond it. He also realizes this makes him a target.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa sees his unrankable status and feels a flicker of something other than rivalry — not pity, but recognition of something different. She says nothing, but her attitude shifts slightly.
- **Dark/World-building:** Reveals that Neural Link above A is theoretically possible but extremely rare. No cadet in recent memory has exceeded A. The system is designed to cap at A because higher percentages were considered theoretical. Silas's unrankable status suggests something unusual.

### Chapter 5 — Squad Assignments
- **Beat (Plot):** Squad assignments are announced. Silas and Tessa are placed in the same squad — the "Crimson Squadron." Their squad is a mix of inner and outer planet cadets, creating immediate political tension. An inner planet cadet (the squad leader) resents Tessa's placement.
- **Character (Silas):** Starts noticing individual cadets as people, not just variables. He notices a border planet cadet in his squad — someone from a similar background. They share a moment of quiet recognition.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Forced proximity. They're squadmates now, which means they have to work together regularly. Their dynamic shifts from rivals to teammates who still disagree but must coordinate.
- **Dark/World-building:** Introduces squad structure — how cadets are organized into combat units. The academy philosophy: "You learn to fight with the people you'll deploy with." Squad cohesion is graded.

### Chapter 6 — The Instructor
- **Beat (Plot):** Cadets are assigned instructors. Instructor Kael — a veteran of border skirmishes — takes particular interest in Silas. He notices Silas's Tactics brilliance but also his Physical weakness. He gives Silas a warning: "Your mind is sharp. Sharp minds get you killed if your body can't back them up."
- **Character (Silas):** Instructor Kael's words resonate. He begins to understand that his Physical Endurance isn't just a weakness — it's a liability that could get him and others killed during high-speed neural link flight.
- **Relationship (Silas/Kael):** Mentorship begins. Kael sees something in Silas that others don't — a raw talent that needs guidance, not punishment.
- **Dark/World-building:** Instructor Kael has fought in actual border conflicts. He knows what happens when a pilot's Neural Link outpaces their Physical Endurance. He warns cadets about a phenomenon: when the craft reacts faster than the body can process, the pilot experiences sensory overload, G-force effects, and in extreme cases, hallucinations — including mistaking friendlies for foes.

### Chapter 7 — Neural Link Incident
- **Beat (Plot):** During a high-stakes simulation, Silas's Neural Link spikes uncontrollably — his starfighter reacts INSTANTLY to everything, faster than any cadet should be able to react. The reaction is so violent it causes a system disruption. Tessa is nearly hit by friendly fire — she instinctively dodges and saves herself. The simulation is aborted.
- **Character (Silas):** Terrified. He realizes his Neural Link isn't just "above average" — it's dangerous. When it spikes, his starfighter moves with a reflex speed that could kill his squadmates if they were real.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa's instinct saved her. Silas's calculations couldn't have predicted her move. He's forced to confront the reality that instinct has a value his calculations cannot capture. For the first time, he doesn't try to quantify it — he just acknowledges it.
- **Dark/World-building:** The simulation system registers Silas's Neural Link at an abnormal level — "CRITICAL: LINK EXCEEDS MAXIMUM" appears on the display. Instructors are alarmed. This is not just a high rating — it's a potential safety hazard.

### Chapter 8 — The Aftermath
- **Beat (Plot):** Following the simulation incident, Silas is pulled aside by the academy's lead instructor. He's given a choice: reduce his Neural Link output to safe levels (which means intentionally flying below his capability) or accept increased Physical training to build his body's ability to handle the reflex speed his Neural Link creates. He chooses training.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins his Physical Endurance training with intensity. He's still near the bottom of the class, but now he has a reason — not just to pass, but to survive what his own power creates. He starts to understand the ticking clock: every time he pushes his Neural Link harder, he needs his body to keep up.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They share a quiet moment after the incident. Tessa says: "Your calculations were perfect. But I'm not a variable you can predict." Silas responds: "Maybe I need to learn to predict you." It's not romantic — it's tactical. But it's also the first time he's trying to understand someone as an individual, not a variable.
- **Dark/World-building:** The incident is logged. Other cadets start to avoid Silas during flight practice. He's isolated — not just by his Physical weakness, but by the fear of what he can do. The academy considers restricting his access to advanced simulations.

## ACT II: THE GRIND (Chapters 9–20)

### Chapter 9 — Training Montage
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas pushes through brutal Physical Endurance training. He's exhausted, injured, but making progress. Tessa notices — she doesn't offer sympathy, but she doesn't mock him either. She starts staying late to help him with flight simulations, using her instinct-based approach to complement his Tactical knowledge.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins to understand that growth isn't about being perfect at everything — it's about compensating. His Tactics are his strength; his Physical is his weakness; his Neural Link is his secret weapon and his greatest danger.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They develop a working rhythm. Silas calculates; Tessa improvises. In simulations, their combined approach is more effective than either alone. Neither admits it, but they're getting better together.
- **Dark/World-building:** Introduction of the "Border Cadet" program — outer planet and border planet cadets who fight a war the inner planets deny exists. Silas hears rumors of alien attacks in deep space. Some border cadets speak of it; inner planet cadets dismiss it as propaganda. Silas doesn't know who to believe.

### Chapter 10 — The First Tournament
- **Beat (Plot):** First academy-wide tournament — a multi-stage competition testing Tactics, Physical Endurance, and Neural Link integration. Silas qualifies for the early rounds through Tactics alone, then struggles in Physical rounds. Tessa dominates Physical rounds but needs Silas's Tactics to strategize for the Neural Link rounds.
- **Character (Silas):** Learns to trust Tessa's instinct in combat situations. He calculates the odds; she makes the call. Together, they advance further than either would alone.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** The tournament forces them to rely on each other. During a critical match, Silas's Neural Link spikes dangerously — Tessa overrides his strategy with an instinctive maneuver that works. Afterward, she says: "You see the board. I see the move. We need both."
- **Dark/World-building:** The tournament reveals the academy's competition structure — how cadets advance through rankings, how top performers earn privileges, how the bottom performers face consequences (reassignment, public humiliation, squad demotion).

### Chapter 11 — Rivalry Escalates
- **Beat (Plot):** A rival cadet — an inner planet legacy, high Neural Link rating, arrogant — challenges Silas publicly. The rivalry is political (border vs. inner planet) and personal (Silas's unrankable status threatens the rival's sense of hierarchy).
- **Character (Silas):** Realizes his presence at the academy is political. He's not just a cadet — he's a border planet presence in an inner-planet-dominated institution. This makes him a target.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa defends Silas against the rival's provocation. It's not out of pity — it's out of respect for his skill. She says: "He's not the problem. You are." To Silas, this means something.
- **Dark/World-building:** The academy's politics are laid bare. Inner planet families have connections to instructors. Border planet cadets are often viewed as "charity cases." The civil war tension seeps into the academy culture.

### Chapter 12 — Late Night Discovery
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas stays late in the simulation lab, running private tests on his Neural Link. He discovers that when he pushes his link to maximum output, his starfighter can perform maneuvers that are physically impossible for any standard craft — but the G-forces are lethal. He can only sustain it for seconds before his body shuts down.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins to understand the scale of his power. It's not just "high" — it's orders of magnitude beyond any trained pilot. But it's also a liability he can't control yet. Every time he pushes it, he risks unconsciousness or friendly fire.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa catches him running these tests. Instead of reporting him, she helps him analyze the data. She's fascinated by what he's discovered. They spend hours together, and for the first time, Silas doesn't see her as a variable — he sees her as a collaborator.
- **Dark/World-building:** The simulation lab has restricted access. Silas shouldn't be there. Tessa shouldn't either. Their secret research session establishes a bond that's intellectual and emotional — not romantic, not yet.

### Chapter 13 — Squad Dynamics
- **Beat (Plot):** Crimson Squadron prepares for a group mission — a simulated patrol of border space. The squad must work together, integrating individual strengths. Silas and Tessa's combined approach proves superior, but friction emerges when other squad members question their decisions.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins to see the squad as a team, not just a collection of variables. He starts noticing individual cadets' strengths and weaknesses — not for tactical optimization, but because he cares about their performance.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They're the squad's tactical core. Silas plans; Tessa executes. The dynamic is working, but it's creating tension — other cadets feel sidelined. Silas notices this and starts advocating for his squadmates, showing growth in his "people as variables" flaw.
- **Dark/World-building:** The simulation mirrors real border patrols — where outer planet cadets report alien contacts that inner planet command dismisses. Silas hears a border cadet describe an attack in detail. It's specific, credible, and terrifying. But no one believes it.

### Chapter 14 — The Challenge
- **Beat (Plot):** The rival cadet challenges Silas to a one-on-one duel in the main arena. It's a public event, streamed to the entire academy. The stakes: if Silas wins, he earns a spot in the advanced squadron. If he loses, he's relegated to basic training.
- **Character (Silas):** Faces a choice — use his Neural Link and risk a dangerous spike, or fight within his current capabilities. He chooses to fight within his limits, relying on Tactics and physical endurance he's been building.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa watches from the crowd. When Silas's Neural Link begins to spike mid-fight, she instinctively reacts from her seat — mouthing instructions based on her reading of the situation. Silas follows them without knowing why. They win.
- **Dark/World-building:** The duel demonstrates the academy's competition philosophy — "in war, you can't let your guard down." It's not lethal, but it's brutal. Injuries are common. The academy frames it as "training for reality."

### Chapter 15 — The Advance
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas advances to the advanced squadron. He's still physically weak, but his Neural Link rating is now being taken seriously. Instructors begin watching him more closely. Instructor Kael assigns him special training — pushing his Neural Link control while building his Physical Endurance.
- **Character (Silas):** Achieves something meaningful — advancement through his own effort, not luck. But he also realizes this makes him a bigger target. Other cadets resent him; instructors watch him like a hawk.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They're in the advanced squadron together. The dynamic shifts — they're no longer just teammates, they're peers. Tessa's respect for Silas deepens. Silas starts noticing her in a new way — not just as a fellow pilot, but as someone whose instincts are as valuable as his calculations.
- **Dark/World-building:** The advanced squadron is where the best cadets train. It's also where instructors begin preparing cadets for real deployment. Silas overhears instructors discussing "potential threats" — and realizes his name is on the list.

### Chapter 16 — The Alien Threat
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas discovers classified files in the academy's database — reports of alien attacks on outer planet colonies. The reports are detailed, credible, and deeply disturbing. But they're sealed. Only senior cadets and instructors have access.
- **Character (Silas):** Begins to understand that the academy is hiding something. The civil war isn't just political — it's about a threat the inner planets are denying exists. He doesn't know what to do with this information.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa helps him decode the files. She has access through her family connections (her father was an instructor). Together, they piece together the truth: there IS an alien threat, and it's getting worse. The inner planets know — they're just not telling anyone.
- **Dark/World-building:** The alien threat is real. It's not an excuse for independence. But the inner planets are using the denial as political leverage — if the outer planets are being attacked, they need more resources, more autonomy, more military support. The inner planets don't want to give that up.

### Chapter 17 — The Betrayal
- **Beat (Plot):** Someone discovers Silas accessing classified files. He's confronted by a senior cadet who warns him: "Some things you don't look into. They'll get you killed." Silas realizes someone is watching him.
- **Character (Silas):** Learns that knowledge is dangerous at the academy. He's been so focused on training and competition that he hasn't noticed the surveillance. Now he has to choose: keep digging and risk consequences, or stop and ignore what he's learned.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa warns Silas that someone is reporting his activities. They have to be careful — the academy is monitoring them. For the first time, they're operating outside the system together.
- **Dark/World-building:** The academy isn't just a school — it's a intelligence hub. Cadets are monitored, files are classified, and questions are discouraged. The civil war isn't just outer vs. inner — it's information control.

### Chapter 18 — The Crisis
- **Beat (Plot):** During a routine patrol simulation, Silas's Neural Link spikes again — this time in front of his entire squad. He loses control for several seconds. His starfighter performs an impossible maneuver, but he can't control it. He barely avoids colliding with a squadmate's craft.
- **Character (Silas):** The incident terrifies him. He's no longer just dangerous to himself — he's dangerous to his squadmates. He considers withdrawing from advanced flight. But Instructor Kael tells him: "You can't control your link by avoiding it. You control it by facing it."
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa is the one who talks him down after the incident. She doesn't offer sympathy — she offers a solution. "Your link is too fast for your body. Your body needs to catch up. Until it does, you fly with me." She's volunteering to be his co-pilot — to use her instinct to cover his moments of instability.
- **Dark/World-building:** The academy's response to the incident: Silas is pulled from active duty pending review. The lead instructor recommends restricting his Neural Link output. Instructor Kael argues against it — "He's not a problem. He's a weapon. And weapons need to be aimed."

### Chapter 19 — The Choice
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas has a choice — accept Neural Link restrictions (flying at a reduced, safe level) or continue his Physical training and attempt to build his body's capacity to handle his link's output. He chooses the harder path. Tessa commits to training with him.
- **Character (Silas):** Makes a decision that defines him — not the easiest path, not the safest path, but the path that requires growth. He's still calculating, but now he's calculating for something bigger than himself.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They begin training together — late nights in the simulation lab, early mornings in the Physical training gym. It's not romantic, but it's intimate in a different way. They're building something together, and Silas is learning to value the process, not just the outcome.
- **Dark/World-building:** The academy's Physical training is brutal — designed to push cadets to their limits. Silas is already near the bottom; he's now training at an intensity that should be impossible for someone of his starting point. The fact that he's keeping up at all is remarkable.

### Chapter 20 — The Tournament Returns
- **Beat (Plot):** The academy's major tournament — the "Helios Cup" — begins. It's the biggest competition of the year, with top cadets from all squads competing. Silas is eligible to return after his incident, but he's still rebuilding his Physical Endurance. Tessa is one of the favorites.
- **Character (Silas):** Enters the tournament with a clear understanding of his limits — and his potential. He's still physically weak, but his Neural Link is becoming more controlled. His Tactics are sharp. He's ready to fight, even if he can't win every round.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** They both enter the tournament. Their path could cross — and it does. The quarterfinals pit them against each other. It's not personal — it's competition. But it's also personal in a way that goes beyond rivalry.
- **Dark/World-building:** The Helios Cup is more than a competition — it's a selection process. Top performers earn deployment slots, advanced training, and prestigious squadron assignments. Bottom performers face consequences — demotion, reassignment, or expulsion.

## ACT III: THE CLIMAX (Chapters 21–25)

### Chapter 21 — Quarterfinals
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas vs. Tessa — quarterfinals of the Helios Cup. A direct clash of philosophies: calculation vs. instinct. The match is brutal, testing Tactics, Physical Endurance, and Neural Link integration. Silas's calculations are precise; Tessa's instinct is unpredictable.
- **Character (Silas):** In the final moments of the match, Silas's Neural Link spikes. He can't control it — but Tessa instinctively adjusts to cover his instability. They BOTH survive, but neither wins. The match ends in a draw — a first in Helios Cup history.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** The draw forces both of them to confront something uncomfortable: they needed each other to survive. Tessa's instinct saved Silas from his own power; Silas's Tactics saved Tessa from a mistake she would have made. After the match, Tessa says: "You're not just a variable, Silas. You're the most important one."
- **Dark/World-building:** The draw shocks the academy. A quarterfinal match ending in a tie is unprecedented. Instructors are forced to evaluate both cadets on merit, not just performance. The tournament rules are adjusted — but the precedent is set: raw talent can overcome the system.

### Chapter 22 — Semifinals
- **Beat (Plot):** The rival cadet advances to the semifinals. He challenges Silas to a rematch — this time, with the stakes higher. If Silas wins, he gets a direct slot into the elite squadron. If he loses, he's expelled from the academy entirely.
- **Character (Silas):** Faces his greatest challenge yet. He knows he can't win through Physical Endurance alone. He can't win through Tactics alone. He has to trust his Neural Link — and trust that Tessa will be there to cover him if it spikes.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa is in the stands, but she's also in the simulation lab, running data on the rival's flight patterns. She shares her findings with Silas — not as a teammate, but as someone who believes in him. "I don't know if you'll win. But I know you're the only one who can."
- **Dark/World-building:** The rival's background is revealed — his father is a senior instructor. He's not just talented; he's politically connected. The academy's politics are on full display: the rival has been groomed for leadership, while Silas has been fighting for every inch.

### Chapter 23 — The Duel
- **Beat (Plot):** Silas vs. the rival — semifinal duel. The rival uses every advantage — political, tactical, physical. Silas is outmatched in every category except Neural Link. When the rival pushes him to the limit, Silas's Neural Link spikes to maximum. His starfighter performs an impossible maneuver — faster than any craft should be able to react.
- **Character (Silas):** He can't control the maneuver. His body can't handle the G-forces. But he doesn't need to control it — he just needs to SURVIVE it. And he does. The rival is forced to yield. Silas wins the semifinal.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa is in the simulation lab, watching the duel in real-time. When Silas's Neural Link spikes, she instinctively reacts — mouthing the same instructions that saved him in Chapter 14. He follows them. They win TOGETHER. Afterward, she's the first person he sees in the crowd.
- **Dark/World-building:** The rival's neural link spikes in response to Silas's maneuver — he's overwhelmed by the speed difference. He yields, but he's shaken. The academy is stunned. Silas's Neural Link has exceeded anything previously recorded — but it's still unrankable.

### Chapter 24 — Finals
- **Beat (Plot):** The Helios Cup finals — Silas vs. the top-ranked cadet from the inner planets, a Neural Link prodigy rated SS. The match tests everything: Tactics, Physical Endurance, Neural Link control, and most importantly, the ability to work with someone else.
- **Character (Silas):** For the first time, he enters the finals not as an underdog, but as someone who has earned his place. He's still physically weak, but he's made progress. His Neural Link is becoming more controlled. His Tactics are sharp. He's ready.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa is his wingman in the finals. They fly as a team — Silas calculates; Tessa improvises. Together, they outmaneuver the SS-rated opponent. In the final moments, Silas's Neural Link spikes — but this time, he doesn't need Tessa to save him. She trusts him to handle it. And he does.
- **Dark/World-building:** The victory is historic — the first time a border planet cadet has won the Helios Cup. The academy is forced to confront its bias. Inner planet cadets are stunned. Outer planet cadets celebrate. The civil war tension in the academy reaches a fever pitch.

### Chapter 25 — The Revelation (Cliffhanger)
- **Beat (Plot):** Following the tournament, the academy upgrades its Neural Link testing equipment — new technology that can measure beyond the A-rank cap. Silas is required to take the new test. The results are catastrophic: his Neural Link isn't just above A — it's off the scale. The system can't quantify it. The lead instructor reads the results and goes pale.
- **Character (Silas):** Sees the results and understands — he's not just dangerous. He's unprecedented. The system that once couldn't measure him now tells him exactly how far beyond it he is. He doesn't know what to do with that knowledge.
- **Relationship (Silas/Tessa):** Tessa is with him when he sees the results. She doesn't offer comfort — she offers perspective. "You're not a weapon. You're a person. And people grow." For the first time, Silas doesn't see himself as a variable to be managed — he sees himself as someone who can choose his own path.
- **Dark/World-building:** The lead instructor calls an emergency meeting. The alien threat files are unsealed. The outer planet cadets are right — the aliens are real, and they're getting stronger. Silas's Neural Link is the academy's most valuable resource — and its greatest danger. The cliffhanger: the academy is preparing to deploy cadets for the first time in years. Silas is being assigned to the front line.

## KEY NARRATIVE RULES (FROM BEAT SHEET)

1. **Neural Link spikes are TERROR events** — not cool superpowers. When Silas's link goes wild, it should be scary for him AND everyone around him.
2. **Tessa's instinct is NOT magic** — it's pattern recognition + gut instinct + experience. It's real and valuable, but it's not supernatural.
3. **Silas's growth is gradual** — he doesn't wake up seeing people as individuals. He notices it, he resists it, he accepts it. Each step is earned.
4. **The alien threat is REAL but SECRET** — not a conspiracy, just information control. The inner planets know and are using it politically.
5. **Physical training matters** — Silas's growth isn't just mental. His body catching up to his mind is a central theme.
